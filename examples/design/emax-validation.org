#+TITLE: EMAX Validation — Rules and Expected Results
#+STARTUP: showall

* Status
Accepted by the owner (issue #545, PR #552). The rules and the worked example are
written by hand. The scenario tables are generated by =testdata/emax/emax_expected.py=,
which implements these rules in exact arithmetic, shares no code with the Go
engine and never runs it.

Claude wrote this calculator and much of the engine, so a rule misread in both
would still agree. *Reviewing the rules below is what guards against that*, so
review any change to them the same way.

* Configuration
| setting          | value                                        |
|------------------+----------------------------------------------|
| strategy         | =emax=, EMA(2) / EMA(4)                      |
| instrument       | EURUSD, daily bars, weekdays from 2010-01-04 |
| starting balance | $10,000.00, account currency USD             |
| risk per trade   | 1% of equity                                 |
| stop distance    | 1% of the signal bar's close                 |
| margin rate      | 2% (50:1)                                    |
| unit limits      | minimum 1, step 1, no maximum                |
| costs            | none: no spread, commission or slippage      |

* Rules
** Arithmetic
1. Prices are integer points (1.10000 is 110000); money is integer cents; EMAs
   are exact fractions here. Scenario tables round EMAs for reading; the exact
   values are in the last section.
2. P/L is =(exit - entry) * units= for a long and =(entry - exit) * units= for a
   short, rounded *down* once to the cent, per trade, so a fraction of a cent
   always goes against the account: +$33.00024 is +$33.00 and -$102.08316 is
   -$102.09.
3. Unrealized P/L uses the same formula and rounding, at the last close.
4. Equity is balance plus unrealized P/L.
5. Margin is =units * price * 2%=, rounded *up* to the cent.

** Strategy
6. EMA(n) is seeded with the simple average of the first n closes, then
   =EMA += (2 / (n + 1)) * (close - EMA)=.
7. EMAX does nothing until both EMAs are warm, so the first possible order is on
   bar 3 (the fourth bar).
8. *The first entry is not a crossover.* On the first warm bar EMAX enters the
   side the EMAs already show.
9. EMAX emits at most one order per bar, from that bar's close.
10. When the trend differs from its signal state, EMAX emits a close and records
    the new side as pending. On the next bar it emits the open for that side,
    *without re-checking the trend*.
11. *EMAX's signal state is what it last asked for, not the position.* A stop-out
    does not change it. EMAX stays flat until the EMAs cross again, then emits a
    close with nothing to close, then its open. (Decided: keep this for now.)

** Timing, for each bar in order
12. *The open:* market orders from earlier bars fill at this bar's open. An open
    that fills places its stop, which is live at once. Any stop the open is
    already through then fills at the open.
13. *The range:* a long's stop triggers when the low is at or below it and fills
    at the stop. A short's triggers when the high is at or above it and fills at
    the stop. An exact touch triggers.
14. *The close:* the position is marked at the close. Margin rules are checked
    and do not fire in any scenario here.
15. *The strategy* then sees the bar and may submit an order. It can never fill
    on this bar.

** Orders
16. An open is sized when it is submitted, from the signal bar's close and the
    equity at that moment:
    - =distance= is =floor(close / 100)= points, at least 1
    - =stop= is =close - distance= for a long, =close + distance= for a short
    - =risk limit= is =floor(1% of equity / (distance in price))=
    - =margin limit= is =floor((equity - used - reserved) / (close * 2%))=
    - =units= is the smaller limit; in these scenarios that is always the risk limit
    - margin is checked again at the fill price, and always passes in these scenarios
17. *1% is planned risk, not a cap.* Size and stop come from the signal close but
    the entry is the next open, so a stop-out can lose more or less than 1%. A gap
    through the stop loses more. Tables show the exact planned risk, not rounded,
    beside the actual P/L.
18. A close that fills cancels the position's stop. A stop that fills ends the
    position, and a later strategy close is rejected: no position to close.
    That rejection is expected and the run continues.
19. Order ids are =sim-N= in the order the broker issues them, stops included, so
    completion order can differ from id order.

** End of data
20. *No invented fills.* An open position stays open and is reported with
    unrealized P/L at the last close; its stop is reported as still waiting. An
    order that has not filled is reported as still waiting, and an open's reserved
    margin is reported at its reference price.
21. A closed trade is a position that actually closed. An open position is not a
    win or a loss.

* Fixture rules
The script refuses bars that break the first four.
- Timestamps are exactly the weekday sequence from 2010-01-04 at =00:00:00Z=, so a
  duplicate, out-of-order or intraday bar cannot pass. The broker fills an order
  only on a bar strictly later than the one that made it, so the expected results
  depend on this.
- Each bar's open differs from the previous close, so filling at the signal close
  instead of the next open changes the result.
- High and low are strictly outside open and close, so using the wrong field
  changes the result.
- Every warm bar's EMAs differ by at least 5 points, so no result depends on a
  near tie. The engine's EMAs are floats; the comparison must not be close enough
  for that to matter.
- P/L lands on a fraction of a cent, winning and losing, so a change in rounding
  changes the result.
- Stops are tested at the boundary: a one-point near miss (A, C) and an exact touch
  (B1).

* Worked by hand: the shared long entry and its exits
This is independent of the script. Check it against the tables.
- *EMA(2):* bar 1 seed =(1.10000 + 1.10100) / 2 = 1.10050=; bar 2
  =1.10050 + 2/3 * 0.00150 = 1.10150=; bar 3 =1.10150 + 2/3 * 0.00150 = 1.10250=.
- *EMA(4):* bar 3 seed =(1.10000 + 1.10100 + 1.10200 + 1.10300) / 4 = 1.10150=.
- *Signal on bar 3:* 1.10250 is above 1.10150 by 100 points, so a long is requested.
- *Stop:* =floor(110300 / 100) = 1103= points, so =110300 - 1103 = 1.09197=.
- *Size:* =$100 / 0.01103 = 9066.2=, so 9066 units. The margin limit is
  =$10,000 / (1.103 * 0.02) = 453,309=, so risk limits it.
- *Entry:* bar 4's open, 1.10323.
- *Exit at 1.10687 (A, C):* =+364 points * 9066 = +$33.00024=, so +$33.00.
- *Exit at the stop, 1.09197 (B1, B2):* =-1126 points * 9066 = -$102.08316=, so
  -$102.09. The budget is $100.00, and rounding the units down plans exactly
  =9066 * 1103 / 100000 = $99.99798= of it.

* Not covered
Deliberately left out; none of these is exercised by this validation:
- a profitable short closed by a reversal (the rounding and P/L formula are covered
  by D and C's unrealized value);
- an entry gapping past its own stop, and a reversal where the trend turns back on
  the next bar (rule 10);
- margin limits, warnings and closeouts, take profits, trailing stops, limit and stop
  entries, expiry and cancellation, all of which have their own tests;
- orders rejected at sizing: they are not retained anywhere yet, and that decision is
  deferred to the #546 harness.

* Regenerating
From =testdata/emax=:
- =python3 emax_expected.py bars= overwrites the CSVs from the bars in the script.
- =python3 emax_expected.py tables= reads the CSVs and rewrites the tables below.
- =python3 test_emax_expected.py= tests that bad timestamps are refused.

Overwriting bars is fine; review the diff. Never change an expected value only to
make a Go test pass: find out which side is wrong.

The CSVs follow the storage contract beneath =testdata/emax/<scenario>/data/=. Their
=design-bars-v1= header is deliberately not =canonical-v1=, which needs a manifest
this prototype has no use for.

* Expected results
Each scenario reads in this order: purpose, bars, event timeline,
closed trades, final state, then its orders as reference.

The timeline's phases are the steps of a bar in rules 12 to 15:
*Open*, *Range* and *Strategy*. The close marks the position and
produces no event, because the margin rules do not fire here. Events
are listed in the order they happen, so the timeline shows cause and
effect directly. Rows with no fill or no order are checks too: the
stop near miss and EMAX staying flat after a stop-out are expected
behaviour.
