#+TITLE: Architecture Review — Post-EMAX, Pre-Study/Scanner
#+SUBTITLE: Issue #571 · Reviewed against examples/design at main 879ae93 (2026-11-08)
#+STARTUP: overview

* Decision
*Keep the validated EMAX architecture.* No general runner, handler hierarchy, or
production migration is justified yet. Prove one broker-free ATR Study, a
two-symbol observation stream and Scanner, then a two-symbol trading Strategy.
Only then consolidate common delivery and decide the production boundaries.

This is a design review, not an implementation change. The six EMAX fixture
results remain the regression baseline.

* Implemented boundaries — what the code actually does
| Component | Scope | Current responsibility | Review |
|-----------+-------+------------------------+--------|
| =bar= | Generic | One instrument's OHLCV, stamped with its opening time | No interval or end time, so availability cannot be derived yet. |
| =marketData= | Generic | Read one instrument's CSV into =[]bar=; expose =iter.Seq2[bar,error]= | Good prototype; no multi-file/query/coverage API yet. |
| =ema= | Generic | Consume a bar, return a =float64= value once warmed up | Reusable by a Scanner; state is per instance, so one per instrument. |
| =backtest.run= | Trading | Iterate the sequence, propagate data/context/handler errors | Small historical wrapper, but it takes =*trader=, so a Study cannot use it. |
| =trader.onBar= | Trading | Advance Broker for a bar, then call Strategy, then submit an optional Order | Correct single-symbol sequencing. Owns Broker advancement. |
| =strategy.onBar= | Trading | EMAX consumes a bar, tracks EMA signal/pending side, returns =*order= or nil | Trading-only API. Signal state deliberately differs from Account exposure. |
| =broker= | Trading | Queue and execute orders, manage protective exits, mark accounts and enforce margin | Simulation-specific execution; no Study dependency. |
| =account= | Trading | Size Orders, hold Positions, margin/equity/trades, apply fills | Keep inside trading path. USD quote/account assumption remains. |
| =result= | Trading | Read completed trades/Orders/Account and print a stable summary | Proven for EMAX; don't turn into a universal Study result model. |

Actual single-symbol order within a processed bar:
=Broker expiry/open fills/range fills -> trailing stops -> account marks/margin -> Strategy.onBar -> Trader.handleOrder -> Broker.submit -> Account.size=.
An order generated from a bar cannot fill on that same bar. The Broker may
place and trigger a protective exit in an entry bar, per documented OHLC
assumptions. Results leave outstanding Orders/Positions at end-of-data.

* Opening, availability and fill times
These are three different times, and only the first is represented today.

| Time | Meaning | In the code at 879ae93 |
|------+---------+------------------------|
| Opening | Start of the bar's interval | =bar.Time= |
| Availability | When the full OHLC is known; the earliest a consumer may see the bar or decide from it | Not represented: =bar= has no interval or end, and =marketData.interval= is an unparsed string |
| Eligible fill | The earliest an Order may execute | Approximated by =b.After(o.Time)= in =order.execution= |

*Requirement:* a fill's time must not precede the availability of the bar that
caused the Order.

The current check meets this only within one instrument and interval. A
strategy stamps =o.Time= with the opening time of the bar it saw, so =o.Time=
means "open of the signal bar", not "decision time", and the check compares two
opening times. Probe at 879ae93: a EURUSD H1 bar stamped 10:00 (known at 11:00)
produced a GBPUSD market Order, and an offset GBPUSD H1 bar stamped 10:30 then
filled it at 10:30, before the decision could have been made.

Recorded fill timestamps also need a decision before #573–#575 rely on them. A
market fill at the open is stamped correctly. A range-triggered fill happens
later in the bar and a margin closeout executes at the close, but both are
stamped with the bar's opening time. No EMAX change is required.

* Contract decisions for #572–#576
These issues carry the work of =todo.org= TODO 3 (#572, #574), TODO 4 (#573,

** #572 — ATR Study
- Process a deterministic single-instrument bar sequence, without creating
  =trader=, =broker=, =account= or =order=.
- Use a concrete Study with indicator state and a small typed result (instrument,
  timestamp, ATR value); independently verify warm-up, true ranges, smoothing,
  error/cancellation propagation and repeated results.
- #572 comes before #573 decides availability. Until then, report the bar's
  opening time, labelled as such, and prove no look-ahead by showing that the
  ATR for bar N does not change when later bars are added.
- State before implementing: the numeric type (=ema= uses =float64=; say whether
  ATR follows it as a research measurement or stays in fixed-point =price=) with
  its rounding or test tolerance, the first bar's true range when there is no
  previous close, and the seed (SMA of the first N true ranges or Wilder's).
- The previous close across a missing bar depends on #573's missing-bar policy.
- Start by directly iterating =marketData.bars(ctx)= in a focused example/test.
  *Do not* extract a general Study interface or shared runner just yet.
- An Indicator computes measurements; a Study decides what to collect/report.
  A Scanner classifies/selects instruments; a trading Strategy requests Orders.

** #573 — Observation availability and multi-symbol data
- A bar's =Time= currently stores its *open* timestamp. Its full OHLC values
  become known at the end of its interval; event ordering for research
  consumers and decisions must use *availability/completion* time as well as
  distinguish the quoted bar start. Historical fixtures only establish
  next-bar execution on a single D1 stream; they do not validate H1/live
  availability or cross-interval ordering.

Decided and implemented in #573:
- *Availability* is the field =bar.availableAt=, calculated once when the bar is
  built and read by consumers, never recomputed. It is not stored in the
  canonical file for now. In the prototype =parseBar= sets it to open plus the
  interval; only =h1= and =d1= are supported, because weekly and session-based
  bars do not end at open plus a fixed duration. In production the
  raw-to-canonical conversion owns the rule per source. It is expected to be
  stored in the canonical format eventually, which should touch only loading.
- *Bars:* =mergedBars= delivers one =iter.Seq2[bar, error]= ordered by
  =availableAt=, then instrument name, independent of argument order. Delivering
  by availability is the no-look-ahead rule: a D1 bar arrives after that day's
  earlier H1 bars although it opened first. The sets must have different
  instruments and strictly increasing availability, or the sequence reports an
  error.
- *Frames:* =frames= groups bars with the same =availableAt=, in instrument
  order. Frames are fresh-only.
- *Which to use:* a per-instrument consumer can read bars. A consumer that
  relates instruments at one moment needs frames, because between the bars of a
  moment it would see one instrument's new close beside another's old one.
- *Missing:* the instrument is absent from that frame. Nothing is synthesized or
  carried. A consumer that wants a last value keeps its own.
- *Late:* cannot occur in a sorted in-memory replay; out-of-order input is an
  error. Late live arrival is deferred.
- *Frame completeness:* in replay a frame is complete when the next bar is later.
  Live completeness is deferred.
- *Moved to #575:* the fill-time requirement above and the question of whether
  the Broker must advance per frame. #573 changes delivery only.

** #574 — EMA Scanner
- At least two instruments, distinct EMA state per instrument, explicit
  warm-up and deterministic classification output.
- No Account, Broker, Trader, or trading Order. Validate missing-symbol and
  simultaneous-observation behavior using #573's decided delivery semantics.

Implemented in #574: =runEMAScan= reads the merged bars, since each
classification depends on one instrument. Readings carry =availableAt=. Trend is
up, down or flat, with flat meaning the EMAs agree within 1e-9. The tolerance
exists because =ema= is =float64= and a constant 1.10000 series seeds the two EMAs
a rounding error apart. =emax= still compares its EMAs exactly; that is tested
EMAX behavior and is left unchanged, but a consumer built on it inherits the
hazard.

** #575 — Two-symbol trading Strategy
- One Broker and one Account with Positions for both symbols. The present
  =emax= object contains only one pair of EMA states and one signal state, so
  *do not reuse it as a two-instrument strategy without partitioning state*.
- Partitioning must keep the invariant in =single-vs-multi-trader.org=: a shared
  Broker has one market-event advancement path. =trader.onBar= advances the
  Broker itself, so fanning one bar out to two Traders would advance it twice.
  How to compose this is open (decision 7).
- Meet the fill-time requirement across instruments, moved here from #573, taking
  availability only from the delivered bar. Decide whether the Broker advances
  per frame, using #573's =frames=.
- Test two signals at one effective time; decide ordering and whether callback
  returns 0, 1 or multiple Orders. Account equity/margin must remain shared;
  deterministic order admission matters when both signals compete for margin.
- *Known unsafe multi-symbol behavior:* Broker can margin-liquidate a different
  instrument at its older mark while timestamping the close on the new bar.
  Test and define a freshness/executable-quote policy; don't imply a stale
  mark was an actual contemporaneous fill. This was already flagged in
  =todo.org= and is not an EMAX regression.
- *Stale marks affect more than fills, even on aligned data.* =broker.onBar=
  marks only the bar's instrument and then checks margin, so when two instruments
  share a timestamp the check after the first bar values the second at its
  previous close. Margin evaluation, equity and sizing all need a defined
  valuation point. Refusing stale fills is not enough: in the probe below the
  closeout fills at a fresh price and only its trigger is stale.
- Probe at 879ae93, one bar each at the same timestamp, $10,000 account, long
  200,000 units of each from 1.00000, EURUSD closing 0.96500 and GBPUSD 1.03500:
  EUR then GBP gives one closeout and leaves only GBPUSD open; GBP then EUR gives
  no closeout. A separate sizing probe, $10,000 account, long 20,000 GBPUSD from
  1.00000 closing 0.95000, EURUSD open at 1.00000 with a 0.99000 stop: the open
  sizes to 10,000 units before GBPUSD's bar at that timestamp is processed and
  9,000 after it.
- USD-only quote currency and fixed price scaling are *prototype constraints*;
  for two-symbol validation use symbols with compatible account/quote
  currency or explicitly defer conversion. The =instruments= registry holds only
  EURUSD, so #575 must register a second USD-quoted symbol such as GBPUSD;
  =parsePrice= requires five decimals, which rules out JPY pairs at their usual
  three.

** #576 — Shared runner / Dataset API decision
- After the three examples, compare their iteration paths. Extract only
  genuine shared concerns (cancellation, data error delivery, deterministic
  sequencing) into a small function if that reduces total code.
- Keep Broker-before-Strategy sequencing in the trading composition, not in a
  universal market-data runner. Studies/Scanners must not return dummy Orders.
- Explore an *in-memory* Query/Dataset/Load contract with instruments,
  interval, source/basis, [start,end), explicit gap policy and ordered Bars.
  Disk assembly, caching, provider adapters, durable journals and migration
  remain later implementation work.

* Open decisions (do not resolve speculatively)
1. Do strategy callbacks need a read-only Position/Account snapshot? EMAX
   currently intentionally tracks signal state, not exposure.
2. How is frame completeness decided for live data? Replay frames, missing bars
   and carried values were decided in #573.
3. Should multiple Orders be returned from one callback, and in what stable
   priority/order are they admitted for margin?
4. What is the availability rule for weekly and session-based bars, and how does
   availability relate to Broker execution?
5. At what valuation point are margin, equity and sizing evaluated across
   instruments, and how is execution safely inhibited when an instrument has no
   current executable quote, especially during portfolio margin liquidation?
6. What is the minimal dataset ownership/coverage contract before bringing
   over the legacy marketdata code?
7. How does a two-symbol Strategy keep one Broker advancement path: one Trader
   with per-instrument state, each bar routed to exactly one Trader, or Broker
   advancement moved out of =trader.onBar=?
8. What do recorded fill timestamps mean for range-triggered fills and margin
   closeouts?
