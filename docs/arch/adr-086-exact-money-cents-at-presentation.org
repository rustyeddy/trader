#+TITLE: ADR-086: Exact Money, cents only at presentation
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Accepted. Issues #590 (adopt =num= in the root engine) and #591. Applies
ADR-004's rule that an operation needing a rounding policy other than the
default must select and document it. This is the first decision of #590's
decision record; its remaining rounding decisions (risk sizing, margin-level
comparison, composed expressions, the order-notional bound) are still open
and will be recorded by amendment or a further ADR.

* Context

The root engine rounds to cents at three points, each in a chosen direction:

- the margin requirement, rounded up to the cent so it is never understated;
- P&L at close, rounded down to the cent so a loss is never understated;
- printed amounts, shown as whole cents.

=num.Money= is exact at 1e-8 and has no operation that rounds to a
coarser unit. Preserving the root's cent rounding would therefore require
adding a named settlement-rounding operation to =num=, and it would have to
know the currency's minor unit (two decimals for USD, none for JPY, eight
for BTC).

The owner has stated the operating range: account values at most about $1M
typically and $1B at the extreme, and sub-cent precision is not needed.

* Decision

=Money= stays exact throughout the engine. Balances, equity, margin and P&L
are never rounded to cents. A value is floored to cents only when it is
printed, as a presentation step at the report boundary.

=num= gains no settlement-rounding operation and no currency minor-unit
table.

Flooring is toward negative infinity, including for negative amounts: -102.08316
prints as -$102.09, not -$102.08, matching today's P&L floor. The
presentation step is tested for negative values and for amounts on both sides
of a cent boundary.

Exactness does not relax overflow handling. Money arithmetic is checked and
returns errors; panics are reserved for programmer-invariant violations, not
for assumptions about market values or account size.

* Alternatives considered

** Add a named cent-rounding operation to =num=

For example =Money.RoundDownTo= and =RoundUpTo=, in the style of
=Price.RoundDown=. It would reproduce today's multi-trade results to the
cent. Rejected: it grows the public API of =num= against the decision to
avoid unneeded complexity, needs a per-currency minor unit that =num=
otherwise does not have, and leaves three rounding sites in the root to keep
correct, all to preserve sub-cent behavior that the owner has said does not
matter.

** Keep the root's own cent arithmetic behind an adapter

Rejected earlier on #590: =num= deliberately offers no raw-scaled
constructor or accessor, so a root-side cent adapter would be a bridge it
does not provide.

* Consequences

- Five of the six EMAX summaries are unchanged by report-only flooring
  (checked from the fixtures in =emax_backtest_test.go=). *b2-stop-cross*
  is not: it closes one trade and holds an open position, so equity is the
  exact sum 9897.91684 + 35.5758 = 9933.49264 and prints $9933.49, where the
  approved snapshot has $9933.48 (the sum of the two already-floored
  components). Resolved below: equity is printed as the sum of floored components.
- Printed equity is the sum of the floored components, so printed figures add
  up; it can be one cent below the exact equity.
- On a run with many trades, balances may hold sub-cent values. For identical
  fills and sizes, the settlement and display difference from the old engine
  is at most one cent per trade, half a cent on average if the fractional
  parts are uniform (an estimate that depends on the data, not a guarantee).
  That bound does not cover changed decisions: equity and margin feed sizing,
  order admission and liquidation, so a sub-cent difference can change a later
  decision and the full-run divergence can exceed it.
- The margin requirement is no longer rounded up to the cent. It is exact, so
  the conservative direction is lost only below one cent.
- Anything other than the report that reads balances, such as a future API or
  export, sees exact sub-cent values and is responsible for its own
  presentation rounding.
- Results of the converted engine are not byte-comparable with the old engine
  on multi-trade runs. The conversion PR may compare the two on a multi-trade
  backtest and report the divergence. Settlement and display differences are
  at most one cent per trade; larger divergence indicates a changed decision
  and should be explained, not assumed to be rounding.
- Risk and margin decisions must be protected independently of the rounding
  policy: after a quantity is sized and quantized, the planned risk and the
  required margin are verified against their budgets, and the quantity is
  reduced by one increment or the order is rejected if either is exceeded.
  Closeout thresholds use a documented conservative boundary rule. These
  rules are decided with the remaining #590 rounding decisions.
- If exact cent-level reproduction is later required, adding the named
  operation to =num= remains available and is a separate decision.

* Equity presentation (decided)

Under pure per-value flooring the b2-stop-cross summary would print equity
$9933.49 instead of the approved $9933.48. The owner chose to print equity as
the sum of the already-floored displayed components (balance + unrealized),
so the printed figures add up and all six approved summaries stay
byte-identical. The cost is that printed equity can be one cent below the
exact equity. Only the printed value is affected; engine equity stays exact.
This rule is tested with b2 (9897.91 + 35.57 = 9933.48) and with a case where
the exact and summed values differ.
