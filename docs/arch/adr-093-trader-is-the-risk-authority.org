#+TITLE: ADR-093: Trader alone sizes, admits and executes orders
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Proposed (#686). Acceptance is the owner's decision. It builds on accepted
ADR-005, ADR-006 and ADR-066 and amends none of them: it says *who* may request
an order and *where* sizing and risk admission happen, now that Trader has clients
(Investor, Tulip) and external strategies that may be built in other repositories.

* Context

Trader's requirements (=trader-framework-requirements.org=, "Risk Manager") put
gating trades on the account: maximum risk per trade and per account, maximum
margin, position sizing and loss limits per day, week or month. ADR-005 keeps
strategies away from brokers. ADR-006 makes risk admission a separate, auditable
stage and rejects risk inside the strategy because risk is an account-level
concern. In the live tree, =account.size= sizes every open against a risk budget
(1% of equity by default) and available margin, and =marginLevelPolicy= handles
closeout.

Three things now put pressure on this:

- *Investor* (rustyeddy/investor), the AI research layer, has a draft policy in
  which Investor sizes trades, reserves capacity, enforces aggregate, correlated,
  margin, halt and freshness limits, and then submits paper orders to Trader.
- *External strategies* (ADR-062/063) are being brought back as gRPC processes
  that may be built and released from a separate repository (rustyeddy/strategies).
  Trader cannot review that code as part of its own changes.
- *More transports* (REST, MCP, Tulip) mean that any check living in a client is
  advisory, because the next client does not run it.

Two risk engines would disagree, and the one that is skipped is whichever one a
given order did not pass through. Backtests would also stop predicting paper
results, because backtests would run Trader's sizing while paper orders ran
Investor's.

* Decision

** Strategies and authorized agents request orders; Trader owns admission

A strategy (in-process or external under ADR-062), an authorized agent, or an
owner-operated manual source may originate an =order= in =stateRequested= for an
account/run the owner has authorized. The requested order expresses a desired
trade (instrument, action, direction, reference price, stop and optional exits).
This is sometimes called an /intent/, but it is *the requested stage of the
same Order*, not a second domain type to be copied into one. The existing
=orderState= lifecycle (=stateRequested=, =stateSized=, =stateSubmitted=,
=stateFilled=, =stateRejected= and =stateCancelled=) remains authoritative.
A request is something wanted, not an executed trade.

The originator has no authority to choose its account-level quantity, approve
risk, assert a later order state, set a broker/order identifier, or execute.
Trader validates the origin and request, binds the authorized account, controls
all state transitions, and routes the same Order through Account sizing/risk
admission before Broker submission. Any untrusted transport representation is
decoded and validated into a *requested* Order: supplied quantity, state,
admission or execution fields are ignored/rejected, never trusted. A common
Order type does not mean clients may write its internal lifecycle fields.

Sources include:
- *Rule-based strategies*, in-process or external (ADR-062).
- *Agent-discretion*, with or without a conventional strategy process. Agents
  may propose requested Orders only through a deliberately authorized ingress,
  bounded to an account/run, policy and operational limits. Exposing a network
  ingress is a separate implementation/security decision, not approval for
  arbitrary REST/MCP trade submission or client-controlled sizing.
- *Owner discretionary requests* via a protected local mechanism (#693). The
  origin and experiment are recorded. A broker's own interface can bypass Trader
  and is therefore not represented as having passed Trader admission; external
  fills require reconciliation (ADR-079, ADR-081).

An agent's nondeterministic decisions are labeled as forward experiments;
replaying *recorded requested Orders* avoids calling the model again and does
not turn hindsight into a valid historical backtest. A late or unavailable
agent response produces no request. An agent, like a strategy, cannot bypass
the Account/Broker path.

** The owner can halt any strategy or account from the host

A misbehaving strategy is stopped by a halt, not by an opposing discretionary
trade, which on a netting (FIFO) account would change the strategy's position
behind its back. =trader halt= for a strategy or an account stops admitting its
intents and cancels its pending orders. With =--flatten=, it also closes its
positions. Trader performs the halt itself, so it works when a strategy process
is hung. The halt persists across restarts until resumed, and every halt is
journaled as an incident. It uses the same owner-only local channel (#692).

** Trader sizes and admits every order, with one code path

Sizing and risk admission happen in Trader's Account machinery, per account and
across accounts, and only there. An originating strategy or agent supplies a
requested Order (side, reference entry, stop and exits); it cannot specify an
authoritative quantity or widen a limit. Broker submission is permitted only
after Account admission, and the Broker cannot override a rejection. Trader
owns transitions of the same Order through its entire =orderState= lifecycle;
no separate =Intent= struct or order-copying workflow is introduced. The same
admission code runs in backtest, paper and live; modes may require different
configured limits, but cannot silently skip a required one.

** Risk policy is versioned Trader configuration that only the owner changes

Limits come from a versioned risk-policy configuration that names the accounts it
covers. The owner changes it in configuration, not through any API operation. A
missing *required* policy settings block new exposure; an unrecognized policy
field invalidates the configuration rather than silently opening exposure.
Controls that do not yet exist cannot be claimed as active or enforced. The
owner-approved requirements are explicit by mode: simulated research and
practice/demo trading may use the implemented minimum controls, with their
limitations reported; real-money trading is disabled until the additional
controls below and atomic admission are implemented, configured and verified.
Any required control that is unavailable or disabled blocks exposure in that
mode. No unspecified limit silently means unlimited. The policy covers, as
implementation arrives:

| Control | Live tree today |
|---------+-----------------|
| Per-trade planned loss as a fraction of equity | =account.size= (1% default) |
| Initial margin admission (ADR-066) | =account.size= margin limit |
| Margin closeout | =marginLevelPolicy= |
| Aggregate planned loss across open positions and pending entries | not yet |
| Correlated or shared-currency exposure groups, signed per currency | not yet |
| Gross and per-instrument notional, leverage ceiling | not yet |
| Loss halts per session, day, week or drawdown, with resume rules | not yet |
| Maximum age of marks, quotes and conversion rates used for admission | not yet |

Concurrent admission against one account **must** be serialized (#538), so two
orders cannot both use the same capacity. This is a required implementation
condition, not a claim that #538 is already complete.

** Decisions are recorded and readable, not steerable

The requested Order is the beginning of its own audit history. Trader records
its originating strategy/agent/version or manual experiment, authorized
account, requested fields, applicable policy version, input marks, computed
quantity, individual rule results (including unavailable/deferred controls),
admission outcome and subsequent =orderState= transitions. Orders, fills and
positions link to that same identity. Durable persistence and read-only REST
reporting are implementation follow-ups; this ADR defines their required
information, not a claim that storage/endpoints exist today. Clients can read
decisions but cannot alter them after admission.

* Consequences

- Investor removes its independent sizing, capacity reservation and account
  eligibility enforcement (rustyeddy/investor#39, #40). It researches versions,
  requests studies/backtests, reads Trader's decisions and recommends activation.
  If explicitly authorized for agent-discretion experiments, it may originate
  requested Orders but never size, admit, execute or transition them.
- A model may affect trading through an owner-activated strategy version or
  an explicitly authorized agent-discretion run. Both emit requested Orders
  through the same Account sizing and admission path. Model-discretion results
  are evaluated forward, not treated as valid hindsight backtests.
- An external strategy or agent ingress needs an explicit deadline, identity,
  authorization and failure policy. Trader records requested Orders and lifecycle
  decisions so a run can be audited and its requests replayed.
- Simulated and broker demo/practice modes may use the documented implemented
  minimum controls, with unavailable controls visibly reported; this is not
  a statement that missing controls are enforced. Real-money accounts must
  reject new exposure until the additional required controls, #538 and applicable
  cross-account aggregation are implemented and configured.
- No external strategy or agent may weaken risk. Its ingress accepts only a
  requested Order and never trusts account quantity or lifecycle state.
- Trader takes on read-only REST endpoints for risk decisions, orders, fills and
  positions. Each needs its own issue.
- This rules out client-side *admission or broker-order placement*, not the
  submission of authorized requested Orders. No client may approve, veto or
  change a risk decision; owner halts are separate authenticated controls.

* Alternatives Considered

- *Investor enforces risk and Trader executes* (the Investor draft). Rejected:
  other clients bypass it, it duplicates =account.size=, and backtests would not
  apply the paper limits.
- *Both enforce, as defense in depth.* Rejected: two sources of truth, every policy
  change lands twice, and when they disagree no one knows which is right. A client
  may still display Trader's decision.
- *A separate risk-service process.* Rejected for now: admission must be atomic
  with account and order state (#538), and a network hop adds failure modes with
  no present use.
- *Strategies or agents size their own orders.* Rejected by the common
  Account admission boundary (ADR-006). One Order type and its lifecycle do
  not grant its originator control over sizing or state.
