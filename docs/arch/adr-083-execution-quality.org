#+TITLE: ADR-083: Execution quality and slippage
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Accepted. Issue #513. Builds on ADR-079 (the sign convention and the unused
benchmark and slippage fields), ADR-081 (per-trade prices, =fullVWAP=,
=fullPrice=) and ADR-082 (spread cost stays out of net). Capturing Trader's own
order reference at submit time is #524; loading historical quote and mid
benchmarks is #525.

* Context

The ledger records what the broker reported. How an execution compared with a
reference is a different question, and the answer depends on a reference the
broker does not provide: a Trader order price, a quote at submit time, a
trigger, a historical mid. Mutating the append-only ledger (ADR-080) each time
a different reference is chosen would be the wrong trade.

* Decision

** A pure derivation, not a ledger write

=internal/executionquality.Measure(fill, benchmark, options)= reads an
=accountactivity.Activity= and returns a =Result=. It stores nothing, reads no
clock and does not write slippage back to the activity. The =Benchmark=,
=Slippage= and =SlippageCost= fields on =Activity= stay valid for a future
persisted-derivation model; #513 does not populate them.

** The benchmark is explicit and typed

The caller supplies the benchmark; Measure never picks one. A benchmark has a
kind, a positive price, a required source, and optionally the time it was
taken. The kinds answer different questions and survive into the result:

| Kind | Answers |
|---|---|
| order_reference | execution against what Trader requested |
| submit_quote_mid | decision-to-fill, implementation shortfall |
| trigger_price | execution against the semantic stop or limit trigger |
| historical_mid | retrospective approximation, weaker precision |
| broker_full_vwap | execution against the broker's no-guaranteed-execution counterfactual |
| broker_quote | execution against the touch of the broker's own execution quote |

A =historical_mid= must state its bar interval, and no other kind may: a mid
taken from bars is a bar-level approximation and is never presented as a quote.
Quote-level precision is not inferred from bars. Helpers build the two broker
benchmarks from a fill's own reported =fullVWAP= and execution quote (best
ask for a buy, best bid for a sell); they are labelled broker-sourced and an
absent field is an error, not a guess. No benchmark at all gives unknown
slippage, never zero.

** Execution price and its provenance

Precedence, recorded in the value's source:

1. the units-weighted average of the broker's per-trade effect prices, when
   every effect has units and an exact price and the units add up to the fill:
   =sum(price x units) / sum(units)= with exact integers and one half-even
   rounding to 8 decimals;
2. the provider's fill-level price, labelled a non-authoritative fallback (for
   OANDA the top-level =price= is deprecated);
3. unknown.

A partial set of effects is never partly averaged. This resolves the
aggregation ADR-081 deferred.

** Signs

Account-centric, the ADR-079 convention: negative is worse for the trader,
positive better, zero neutral. With =d = execution - benchmark=, a buy has
slippage =-d= and a sell =+d=. A measured zero is a known zero.

** Cost and currency

Slippage multiplied by units is money in the instrument's quote currency. It
is expressed in the account currency only when the quote currency already is
the account currency, or when the caller passes an explicit =Conversion= (rate,
from, to, time, source) running from the quote currency to the account
currency. The conversion multiplies in before a single half-even rounding, and
its provenance is kept on the result and in the cost's method. A cross rate is
never inferred; a missing or mismatched conversion, or an instrument whose
quote currency is not known, leaves the cost unknown while the price-unit
slippage stays known.

** Benchmark age

The result carries =BenchmarkAge = fill time - benchmark time= when the
benchmark has a time. It is negative for a benchmark taken after the fill and
is reported, not hidden. Milliseconds before execution and a one-minute-old
midpoint are different claims even at the same price.

** Per fill, kept out of net

One fill, one execution decision, one benchmark. Per-effect execution quality
would need a benchmark per effect, which no source supplies; the trade
effects stay available for audit. Slippage is an analytical dimension beside
profitability: =SpreadCost= is neither added to nor subtracted from it, and it
is not a term of =tradehistory.Net=.

* Consequences

- Choosing another benchmark is another call, not a ledger change.
- Reports (#515) can show the benchmark kind, its age and the price method
  beside each figure.
- The most useful benchmarks need an order ledger (#524) and stored market
  data with precision metadata (#525); until then the caller supplies them.
- Where a conversion rate comes from remains the caller's responsibility.
