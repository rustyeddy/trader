#+TITLE: ADR-082: Trade reconstruction from account activity
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Accepted. Issue #512. Builds on ADR-079 (the activity model) and ADR-081 (the
fill representation). Slippage and execution quality are #513; the CLI and
reporting are #514 and #515; per-position daily financing is #522.

* Context

The ledger holds what the broker reported, fill by fill. Summaries, analysis
and journal generation need completed trades, and they must stay traceable to
the activities they came from. Reconstruction must be deterministic and must
not turn a missing figure into a zero or a broker's number into one of
Trader's.

* Decision

** A trade is a position cycle

A completed trade is flat, to a position, to flat, for one account,
instrument and side. Many fills may enter and many may exit: scale-in and
scale-out stay one trade. A fill that closes a position and opens the opposite
one belongs to two trades. Provider trade IDs do not define the boundary, so
the view is broker-neutral (Alpaca has no trade IDs). Where the broker gives
per-trade effects, they become the legs of the trade, with their exact prices
and units and the broker trade ID, and they decide how a fill divides between
closing and opening; this also handles a hedging account that holds a long and
a short in one instrument. Without per-trade units the net position decides,
and that division is flagged (=Leg.Split=).

** Matching a close to what it closes

A closing leg that names a broker trade closes only that trade's open units.
A leg that names a trade the cycle never saw open is an orphan, not a claim on
some other trade's quantity, so a fill that reduces trade 7 and closes two
unseen trades leaves trade 7's remaining units open and reports the rest as
orphans. Only a leg with no trade ID (a provider without trade linkage, or a
division derived from the net position) closes in entry order.

How a fill divides between closing and opening trusts the broker over the net
position wherever the broker said something: per-trade units that add up to
the fill's size give the exact division; failing that, a fill whose effects
all point one way (all opened, or all closed or reduced) goes that way as a
whole, because a role is a broker fact even when its units are missing; only
with no usable information does the net position decide, and the leg is then
flagged =Split=.

Missing sizes do not erase known linkage. When a role-only fill names several
trades without units, the leg keeps the sorted set of those trade IDs
(=CandidateTradeIDs=) next to its whole, unapportioned quantity. A closing leg
consumes only lots reachable from that set, in entry order, and anything the
set cannot cover is orphan, so a trade the broker did not mention is never
consumed. An opening leg becomes one pooled lot that remains reachable by any
of its named trades: the reconstruction does not invent per-ID quantities, so
a later named close of one of them may take from the pool.

** Net result and the spread-cost rule

Every amount is signed from the account's point of view.

- GrossPL is the sum of broker-reported realized P/L. It is never recomputed
  from prices; a price-based figure may later be exposed as a labelled check.
- SpreadCost is a first-class economic cost, aggregated and shown, but it is
  an attribution already embedded in the broker's realized P/L (a buy
  executes at the ask, a sell at the bid). Subtracting it again would double
  count.
- Net = GrossPL + Commission + Financing + OtherFees, derived, with its source
  stating that spread cost is already embedded.

A spread-neutral, mid-price P/L is a different starting figure and belongs to
the execution-quality layer (#513), where spread cost can become an explicit
term.

** Unknown is not zero

A component total is known only when every fill that contributes to it
supplied it; a reported zero is known. No partial sum is shown as complete.
"Contributes" is role-aware: realized P/L and financing are realized by the
closing, so a pure entry fill that omits them contributes nothing and does not
make the trade's totals unknown (a value it does report is kept), whereas a
closing fill that omits them does. The execution costs are required of every
fill.
Net depends only on its four components: an unknown SpreadCost leaves Net
known, an unknown Commission, Financing or OtherFees makes Net unknown.

** Reversals and apportionment

Realized P/L and financing are realized by the closing, so on a fill that
spans two trades they belong to the closed trade. Execution costs (spread
cost, guaranteed-execution and other fees, commission) are incurred by both
portions. Broker per-trade values are used exactly where every effect supplies
them. Otherwise a known fill-level amount is apportioned by units, the closing
share rounded and the opening share its exact complement so the parts sum to
the fill, and the total's method names the fills apportioned. If there is no
fill-level amount either, the component is unknown on both trades. A
proportional share is never presented as broker-reported.

** What is outside a completed trade

- Open positions are returned separately with entries so far, partial exits
  and what remains; they are not dropped or force-closed.
- Standalone financing and fee activities are listed as unattributed. The
  ledger does not yet carry OANDA's per-position daily financing, so a day's
  financing cannot be allocated safely (#522).
- An exit with no known entry, as when the data window starts mid-trade, is an
  orphan: it is listed, and the trade it touches cannot claim complete totals.

** Arithmetic and determinism

Quantities and prices are exact. A weighted average price is the sum of each
leg's notional (price times quantity, rounded half to even to 8 decimals)
divided by the total quantity, rounded half to even, marked Derived with that
method; it needs the instrument's quote currency, so it is available for
currency pairs and unknown otherwise.

Reconstruction is a pure function of the set of activities. Input is
validated, an identical repeat is dropped and a conflicting one is an error,
every retained activity's time is canonicalized to UTC so equivalent
representations cannot change the output,
and activities are put in the ledger's total order (time, then activity ID,
numeric for digit IDs) before processing. A trade's ID is its first entry's
activity key and its side, so a reversal fill that opens a trade is that
trade's first entry. Every trade lists the activities it was derived from.

** Package

=internal/tradehistory= imports the standard library and =num=, =instrument=,
=order= and =internal/accountactivity= only, enforced by an allowlist test. It
stores nothing and reads no clock; it is a derived view, not the ledger.

* Consequences

The completed-trade view is stable across providers, and OANDA's linkage adds
legs and precision without shaping the model. Trade financing is only what
fills realized until #522. Currency conversion is not attempted: all amounts
are in the account currency, and a mismatch is an error. Open-position cost
summaries and a price-based P/L check are not provided yet.
