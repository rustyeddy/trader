#+TITLE: ADR-087: Formula and rounding decision table for adopting num in the root engine
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Accepted. Issues #590 (adopt =num= in the root engine) and #594. Approved by
the owner, including the conservative-undersizing sizing contract; the
conversion in #590 implements it.
Builds on ADR-086 (exact Money, cents only at presentation) and applies
ADR-004's rule that an operation needing a non-default rounding policy selects
and documents it. Also refers to ADR-025, 027, 030 and 045.

If accepted, this ADR *partially amends ADR-086*: ADR-086 said closeout
thresholds use a "documented conservative boundary rule". The owner has
decided to accept 1e-8 rate precision in the closeout comparison instead (see
"Margin closeout boundary rule"). The guarantee that is lost is that a
position can be liquidated one rounding step later than an exact comparison
would liquidate it.

* Context

The root engine's money arithmetic is exact and directional: each formula
performs one chosen rounding through =muldiv=. =num= rounds half-even after
every operation and exposes no raw values, so composing =num= operations is
not generally equivalent. In particular =Money.MulRate=, then =Money.DivPrice=,
then =Quantity.RoundDown= can round up across an integer before the floor
reaches it (a ratio of 9122.999999996 becomes 9123). That change is not
accepted, even if the six EMAX scenarios still match.

The reviews on #590 asked for a finite table of formulas, rounding and tests
before any conversion. ADR-086 already decides that =Money= stays exact and is
floored to cents only when printed.

* Decision

** Principles

1. *One final rounding where direction matters.* A quantity that is
   rounded down for safety is rounded down once, at the end, never after an
   earlier rounding that could cross the boundary.
2. *Verify, then protect.* After a quantity is sized and quantized to the
   instrument's step, planned risk and required margin are computed again from
   the quantized quantity. If either exceeds its budget, the quantity is
   reduced by one step and checked again, or the order is rejected with the
   existing errors. This protects the decision even when an intermediate
   rounding went the wrong way, and needs no general exact-ratio framework in
   =num=. The check compares rounded values, so it proves safety only up to a
   stated tolerance, not exactly (see "Rounding tolerance").
3. *Exact money.* Balance, equity, margin and P&L are exact =Money=. Cents
   appear only at presentation (ADR-086).
4. *Checked arithmetic.* Every =num= operation returns an error that is
   handled. Overflow is rejected with a clear error, never wrapped,
   saturated, or turned into a panic by an assumption about market values or
   account size. Panics are reserved for programmer-invariant violations.
5. *No raw scale in the root.* The root never imports =num/internal/fixed= and
   never holds a raw scaled integer.

** Formula table

All amounts are in the account currency, which must equal the instrument's
quote currency (see Currency below).

*Input precision.* Prices have five decimals. Rates have six decimals
(=rateScale= is 1e6, and the EMAX strategy accepts any stop percentage between
0% and 100%, for example 0.0004). Units are whole numbers. A product of a
five-decimal price and a six-decimal rate has up to eleven decimals, so it is
*not* exact at =num='s 1e-8 scale. Only some products are exact: a price times
a rate of up to three decimals, a whole-unit quantity times a price, and the
EMAX defaults (a 1% stop, a 2% or 5% margin rate).

| Formula | Inputs | Exact value | Final rounding | Quantum |
|---------+--------+-------------+----------------+---------|
| Stop distance (=stopFor=) | entry Price, stop Rate | entry x stopPct | down to the tick; at least one tick | one tick |
| Risk units | equity Money, riskPct Rate, stop distance Price | (equity x riskPct) / distance | down, then verified | instrument unit step |
| Margin units | margin available Money, price Price, marginRate Rate | (available / marginRate) / price | down, then verified | instrument unit step |
| Order units | min(risk, margin, caps) | as above | down | unit step; reject below instrument minimum |
| Required margin (=marginFor=) | units, Price, marginRate | (price x units) x marginRate | none in engine (one half-even rounding at 1e-8) | none; cents at presentation only |
| Used margin | sum over positions | sum of required margin at the mark | none; checked sum | none |
| Reserved margin | sum over pending opens | sum | none; checked sum | none |
| Margin available | equity, used, reserved | equity - used - reserved | none; may be zero or negative | none |
| Realized P&L | exit and entry Price, whole units | (exit - entry) x units, sign by direction | none (exact) | none; floor at presentation (toward negative infinity) |
| Unrealized P&L | mark and entry Price, units | (mark - entry) x units | none (exact) | none; floor at presentation |
| Equity | balance, unrealized P&L | balance + unrealized | none; checked sum | none; printed as the sum of the floored components (ADR-086) |
| Margin level threshold | equity, used, level Rate | equity < level x used | see boundary rule | none |
| Margin admission at fill | units, fill Price | required margin <= margin available | exact comparison | none |
| Notional admission at fill | units, fill Price | units x price <= max order notional | exact comparison; an over-limit fill is rejected, never resized | none |

*Operation order.* Round the large-magnitude value, not a small per-unit
factor. Multiply the whole-unit quantity by the price first (exact), then apply
the rate once. For units, apply the rate to the money first (=Money.MulRate=
for risk, =Money.DivRate= for margin), then divide by the price with
=Money.DivPrice=. This keeps the one rounding on a value of order
account-currency size, instead of on a per-unit cost such as
0.04999 x 0.0004 = 0.000019996, which =num= would round to 0.00002000 and then
size 50,000 units where the exact value is 50,010. It needs no new =num=
operation.

*Stop distance.* =Price.MulRate= rounds half-even at 1e-8 before the floor to
the tick, so a product just below a tick boundary can round up to it:
0.04999 x 0.0004 = 0.000019996 floors to one tick (0.00001) exactly but becomes
two ticks after rounding. The stop distance is not a safety quantity, because
sizing uses the actual distance between the order's price and its stop, so the
ADR accepts this: the stop can be at most one tick wider than the exact floor,
only when the exact product is within 0.5e-8 below a tick boundary. This case
is pinned in the tests. The EMAX defaults are exact and unaffected.

The sizing formulas use the order's price at submission. The admission check
uses the fill price. These can differ for resting orders; the table keeps both
as they are today.

*Signed values.* =num.Price= and =Quantity= are non-negative, so a signed
price difference (stop distance, P&L delta, ATR true range) is ordered with
=Cmp= first or computed in =Money=. P&L keeps its sign and is never rounded
toward zero: at presentation it floors toward negative infinity, so a loss is
not understated.

*Analytics.* Indicators such as ATR stay at the ADR-045 =Float64= boundary.
Their output is analytical, and becomes authoritative only through checked
construction.

** Rounding tolerance

Budget checks compare values that were rounded half-even at 1e-8, so they do
not prove exact non-overshoot. For example 9.99999 x 0.000001 =
0.00000999999 rounds to 0.00001000, so a one-unit order at a 0.00001 stop
distance passes the check although it exceeds the exact budget. The ADR does
not claim mathematical strictness. It accepts and bounds the tolerance:

- With the operation order above, a budget or required margin is one
  rounding of a value of order account-currency size, so it differs from the
  exact value by at most 0.5e-8 of the account currency. It does not grow with
  the number of units.
- A sized quantity can therefore exceed its exact budget by at most that
  amount, which is far below the display quantum (one cent).
- A rounding can also drop a quantity by one step when the exact quotient is
  within rounding error of an integer step (9122.999999996 can become 9123 or
  fall below it). Conservative undersizing of this kind is accepted rather than
  building a compound exact-arithmetic API in =num=.

*Amended contract.* The sizing contract is "safe within the tolerance and
tight except at integer-step edges", not "the exact largest safe size". The
existing test =TestAccount_Size_Open_RiskIsBoundedAndTight= is amended to say
so, and keeps its tightness check for ordinary inputs.

** Margin closeout boundary rule

Owner decision. The closeout comparison uses =num= at 1e-8 *rate* precision:
the margin level is =equity / used= as a =Rate= rounded at 1e-8, and the
position is liquidated, or warned, only when that level is strictly below the
threshold. Equality is healthy, as today.

Because the precision is on the ratio, the equity-level difference from an
exact comparison scales with used margin: at most 0.5e-8 x used. With $1M of
used margin that is $0.005. It is not a fixed 1e-8 dollar tolerance. This
amends ADR-086's conservative-closeout wording (see Status). It is tested at,
one unit below and one unit above the boundary, and the tests record the
tolerance rather than claiming exactness.

** Limits

Numeric representation limits and business limits are separate.

- *Numeric.* =Price.MulQuantity= and the =Money= operations return
  =ErrOverflow= or =ErrUnderflow=. An order, mark or aggregate that would
  overflow is rejected or surfaced as an error and tested, including the
  intermediates and the final result. =num='s range is about 92.2 billion whole
  units, narrower than the prototype's cent-based range.
- *Business (owner decision).* A maximum order notional of **$50 million
  USD-equivalent** is a configurable account risk and trading policy, chosen
  for risk reasons and not to make one multiplication fit. It replaces the
  global =maxOrderUnits=. Provider and venue constraints (minimum and maximum
  units, unit step, tick) stay separate, on the instrument. =maxPrice= remains
  a data-sanity limit on bars and orders.
- $50 million is far inside the =Money= range, so a single order cannot
  overflow. Aggregates can still: summed positions, reserved margin and large
  market moves are checked and rejected or surfaced as errors, never assumed
  impossible. A $1B account at 50:1 would be $50B notional, which the policy
  limit rejects well before the numeric limit.

** Currency

The account has a currency and each instrument has a quote currency, both
explicit, USD only for now. A =Money= with a zero or invalid currency, or an
account and instrument currency mismatch, is rejected with
=ErrCurrencyMismatch= or the corresponding error, and is tested including a
non-USD quote against a USD account. Conversion (=Money.Convert=) is out of
scope.

** Parsing

=parsePrice= keeps its five-decimal and range checks as validation against the
instrument's tick size, outside =num=. =num.ParsePrice= accepts up to eight
decimals; the five-decimal rule is an instrument rule, not a =num= rule.

* Test plan

- *Independent reference.* Each formula in the table has a reference
  calculation in tests only, using =math/big.Rat=, with the same single final
  rounding. The tests compare the production result against the reference and
  never against the production code itself.
- *Boundaries.* Targeted cases on both sides of every integer-unit and cent
  threshold, including the near-integer risk-sizing case (9122.999999996), a
  value exactly on a step, one step above and below, positive and negative P&L,
  zero and invalid currency, equality at the closeout threshold, one unit
  either side, and the maximum and minimum representable values.
- *Post-quantization check.* A case where the sized quantity exceeds its risk
  or margin budget after quantizing is reduced by one step or rejected. The
  tolerance case is pinned: equity 9.99999, riskPct 0.000001, stop distance
  0.00001, whose budget rounds from 0.00000999999 to 0.00001000.
- *Input precision.* Cases with six-decimal rates: 0.04999 x 0.0004 for the stop
  distance (exact floor one tick, accepted result up to two), and $1 available
  margin at price 0.04999 and margin rate 0.0004 (exact 50,010 units, which
  the operation order must reproduce).
- *Tightness.* The amended tight test shows that one more step breaches a
  budget or cap, except at the pinned integer-step edge.
- *Overflow.* Maximum price times maximum permitted units is rejected with a
  clear error and never wraps, saturates or panics, for intermediates and
  results.
- *Presentation.* Negative amounts floor toward negative infinity; equity is
  printed as the sum of the floored components; all six EMAX summaries stay
  byte-identical (a necessary check, not a sufficient one).
- *Fuzz.* A fuzz target for the sizing path against the reference, in the style
  of the =num= fuzz targets.

* Consequences

- No =num= API addition is proposed. The conservative closeout rule and a
  compound exact-arithmetic API were considered and declined for simplicity.
  The conversion reproduces prototype rounding only where it matters, not to
  the last marginal unit.
- Some =error= returns spread through sizing, margin policy and the report.
  Aggregation of balances and P&L returns errors rather than panicking, so the
  signatures of =equity=, =unrealizedPnL=, =usedMargin= and =marginAvailable=
  change.
- A sub-cent difference between the engine and the old prototype can change a
  later sizing, admission or liquidation decision, so divergence on long runs
  is not bounded by one cent per trade.
- The conversion is one coupled change and a single PR, with Market Data and
  instrument promotion kept out.

* Owner decisions recorded

- Closeout comparison at 1e-8 rate precision, strictly below the threshold,
  equality healthy; amends ADR-086's conservative-closeout wording.
- Maximum order notional of $50 million USD-equivalent, configurable as
  account risk and trading policy; provider constraints stay separate.

* Also approved

- Conservative undersizing and the amended "safe within tolerance, tight
  except at step edges" sizing contract, instead of a new exact-arithmetic API.
