#+TITLE: ADR-090: Raw to canonical bars (stage 4)
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Proposed, with the owner's answers of 2026-11-14 applied (below), for final
approval. Issue #628; stage 4 of #584. Builds on ADR-020 (historic data: raw is
immutable, canonical is rebuilt from it), ADR-088 (the instrument catalog) and
ADR-089 (layout, OANDA raw store, package boundary), as amended by ADR-091 and
the proposed ADR-092. It was implemented while it was Proposed; what follows records what was
built and run.

The bar-availability design the owner recorded on #619 (a bar has an opening =Time=
and an =AvailableAt=; no stored close time; zero means not established) is taken as
given.

Implementation (2026-11-18): the canonical package, the public =Bar=, the provider
mapping, the domain service and =trader data build= are built and merged (#631,
#636, #639, #650), and so is the loader (#647): =marketdata/service.Load= and
=trader data status= refuse a partition with bad records unless the caller allows
it, and never use a stale one. Since then: the download goes through the application
service and =cmd/trader= imports no provider (#641, #663); the loader checks the
interior of a series against the FX calendar (#534, section 7); =Verify= checks a
file against its raw (#535, section 8); =trader data audit= reports a range in one
answer (#536, section 9); and the raw archive was migrated to this layout (#642,
ADR-092). On 2026-11-18 (an operator run on the owner's machine, which CI cannot establish) the
owner's archive was migrated, rebuilt as canonical-v2 for
the 26 OANDA FX instruments at =h1= and =d1= (0 failed partitions) and audited
against raw with no findings. The v1 spread cross-check of section 6 is not built:
section 8 is its replacement, checking against the raw itself, and the v1 files are
to be deleted. Acceptance of this ADR is the owner's decision (#643).

* Context

Checked read-only on the owner's machine:

- =/srv/data/canonical/oanda= holds 3,722 =canonical-v1= files (2,400 =h1=, 1,322
  =d1=; no =m1= or =h4=): a comment line, one JSON metadata line (=raw_fingerprint=,
  versions, =bar_count=, first and last bar, =revision=), the columns
  =time,open,high,low,close,avg_spread,max_spread,ticks=, then one row per bar
  present. The archived converter took open, high, low and close from the raw
  record's *bid* prices, kept only the mean and the largest of the four corner
  spreads (ask minus bid), and so dropped the ask prices. It classified each
  record (accepted, incomplete, suspicious, rejected) and *failed closed*: if any
  record in a partition was suspicious or rejected, the build returned an error
  before publishing anything (=firstBadOutcomeError=, =build_normalize.go=), so a
  partition was either fully clean or absent, and the reason lived only in the
  error.
- The live engine reads =design-bars-v1= and derives =availableAt= as open plus a
  fixed duration. That is wrong for OANDA's session day, which opens 17:00 New York
  and lasts 23 or 25 hours across a DST change.
- The raw store (ADR-089) holds, per partition, every bid and ask open, high, low
  and close, the volume and the provider's =complete= flag.

* Decision

** 1. A new schema, canonical-v2

Canonical keeps *everything raw knows about prices* and adds the availability time
the engine needs. A new schema, not the old one: =canonical-v1= could not carry the
ask prices or an availability time, and raw is complete and local, so the existing
files are rebuilt from raw (local, no network) rather than migrated. =canonical-v1=
files are not read by the new code.

The =Bar= and =OHLC= types below are the public =marketdata.Bar= and
=marketdata.OHLC= (#633, ADR-091), not types of the canonical package; they also
carry an optional =Trade= series and an =OHLC()= method that picks the preferred
series (mid, trade, bid, ask). canonical-v2 stores bid, ask and mid only and
refuses a bar with a trade series; persisting trade needs its own schema decision.
The snippet shows the fields canonical-v2 stores.

#+begin_src go
type OHLC struct {
    Open, High, Low, Close num.Price
}

type Bar struct {
    Time        time.Time // the bar's opening instant, UTC
    AvailableAt time.Time // earliest time the completed bar may be consumed

    Bid OHLC
    Ask OHLC
    Mid OHLC // derived once when the bar is built, not computed on every use

    Volume int64 // the provider's activity count, not a tradable quantity
}

func MidOf(bid, ask OHLC) (OHLC, error) // exact midpoint, field by field
#+end_src

File: a comment line =# schema=canonical-v2 provider=oanda symbol=EURUSD
interval=h1 year=2024 month=01=, one JSON metadata line, any bad-record lines (3
below), the columns

#+begin_example
time,available_at,bid_o,bid_h,bid_l,bid_c,ask_o,ask_h,ask_l,ask_c,mid_o,mid_h,mid_l,mid_c,volume
#+end_example

and one row per good bar, in ascending time. Bid and ask are exact text with the
raw store's five decimals and mid with six (the midpoint of two five-decimal prices
can end in a half pip), never through a float. Times are UTC, RFC 3339.

*Mid is a stored field, derived once (owner decision of 2026-11-14, review of
#631).* Mid costs four more prices per bar and is computed once when the bar is
built, not on every use; and some sources give a single price, which can then be
written to the series it is. The rules, enforced by =Bar.Validate= and the file
reader:

- Each of =Bid=, =Ask= and =Mid= is optional: an absent series is the zero =OHLC=
  (four empty fields in the file; a series with only some fields empty is an
  error). At least one series must be present, and every present one must be
  valid (positive prices, high and low containing open and close).
- When both =Bid= and =Ask= are present, no ask is below its bid (a crossed bar is
  rejected), and =Mid= must be present and exactly equal to =MidOf(Bid, Ask)=: the
  exact midpoint at open, high, low and close. =MidOf= is what the builder calls;
  a stored mid that is not that midpoint is malformed. Half a pip is representable,
  so nothing is rounded.
- When a source gives one series, or only one side, there is nothing to derive or
  cross-check: the builder writes the price to the series it knows it is and leaves
  the others absent. Which series a given source writes is decided in that
  source's mapping, not here.
- A candle's mid high is the midpoint of the bid high and the ask high, which need
  not equal the high of a tick-by-tick mid series; it is the midpoint of the candle
  values the provider gives. Open and close are preserved on both sides. The
  spreads of the old format are derivable (=Ask.x - Bid.x=) and are not stored.

** 2. Availability is stored, modeled, and versioned

=available_at= is written at build time as the bar's scheduled end: the next
interval boundary after the opening time. For =h1= that is the next hour; for =d1=
the next 17:00 America/New_York, so a DST day is 23 or 25 hours. This is a
*modeled* availability under a named policy (=scheduled-close-v1=), never presented
as an observed provider timestamp; the metadata records the policy name. A stored
bar always has a nonzero =available_at= not before =Time=. The raw =complete= flag
stays in raw as the provider's own fact.

The policy name carries its version and is stored in the file's metadata
(=availability_policy=); that is the only place the rule's version lives, and a
file's =available_at= values always belong to the policy it names. A reader honors
what the file says and never recomputes. Changing the interval or session rule
(including the New York calendar) means a *new policy name* and a rebuild: a file
naming a policy other than the one in force is stale, the build replaces it (4
below), and a loader reports it rather than using it. Rebuilding is cheap because
canonical is derived (ADR-020).

** 3. Bad records are written into the file, never silently lost

This is a *deliberate change from the archived builder*, which failed closed and
published nothing for a partition with any bad record. The owner chose (2026-11-14)
to publish the good rows and keep the bad ones visible, so a partition may be
partial. The safety invariant that makes this acceptable: *no consumer may treat a
partition with =bad_count > 0= as complete data.* The reader returns the bad
records with the bars; the loader that feeds the engine (a later stage) refuses a
partition with bad records unless the caller explicitly allows known-bad
partitions, and says which; the gaps and coverage stage reports them. Nothing
downstream sees only the good rows by default.

The build writes every good row and *also records every record it rejected*, in the
same file, as comment lines before the column header:

#+begin_example
# bad time=2024-01-05T14:00:00Z reason=crossed raw_row=1203
# bad time=2024-01-05T15:00:00Z reason=incomplete raw_row=1204
#+end_example

=reason= is one of =incomplete= (the provider had not closed the candle),
=crossed= (an ask below its bid at some corner), =invalid= (a high or low not
containing open and close, or a non-positive price), =duplicate=, =out-of-order= and
=misaligned= (not on the interval's boundary). =raw_row= is the row's position in
the raw file: 1-based, counting data rows only (the raw file's schema comment and
column header are not counted), so the first data row is =raw_row=1=. The metadata line carries
=bar_count= and =bad_count=. The canonical reader returns the good bars *and* the
bad records, so a later stage (gaps, coverage) can tell "known bad" from "missing"
from "closed market" and a bad month is never mistaken for a quiet one.

A raw row that cannot even be parsed (the raw reader's =ErrMalformedData=) is not a
bad record: the raw file itself is damaged, so that partition's build fails and
writes nothing. A candle the downloader declined to store because it was still
forming never reaches raw (the owner chose this on 2026-11-14: raw files do not
record it, only the download run's output does), so canonical sees it only as an
absent bar. A download made after the candle has closed adds it, so this heals on
the next run. The gaps stage treats an absent bar at the end of a downloaded range
as possibly still forming, not as proof of a gap. Records with the same time keep the first as the good row and
record the later ones as =duplicate=.

Alignment: =h1= on the hour; =d1= at 17:00 America/New_York, using America/New_York
zone bytes pinned in the repository (loaded from the bytes, never by name, so the
host's zone data cannot change a stored time; changing them is a new policy name).
One small function, tested at the DST and month boundaries; no
=Calendar= interface. Nothing is filled. Whether a gap is an error is decided when
bars are read, by the calendar of section 7.

A forming candle that an older download stored in raw shows as a bad record
(=incomplete=) in its canonical month. It is not left there: the download replaces
the stored forming row with the closed candle (ADR-089), and the next build clears
the bad record.

** 4. Rebuilds and fingerprints

The metadata records =raw_fingerprint= (the raw partition's bytes), the
=builder_version=, the =validator_version= (the accept and reject rules of 3) and
the =availability_policy=. Any change to what is accepted, rejected or computed
bumps the validator or builder version. Canonical is derived, so unlike raw it may
be replaced: a build whose four values match the file on disk does nothing;
otherwise the file is rewritten atomically (temporary file, sync, rename) under the
per-partition lock, and the report says it was replaced. A build never touches raw.

** 5. Packages and the command

The flow (owner-approved on #639, 2026-11-15; layering accepted as ADR-091):
=cmd/trader= supplies configuration to the application service (package =service=,
=service/marketdata.go=); the service reads a raw partition through =provider/oanda=,
which parses and maps it to a =marketdata.SourcePartition=; the service hands that to
the domain service =marketdata/service=, which applies every domain rule and
publishes the canonical file through =marketdata/internal/canonical= and the =store=
package. Where the mapping lives is settled: the provider maps raw rows to
unvalidated =SourceRow= values and the domain service decides what each row is.

- =marketdata= (public): =OHLC=, =Bar=, =MidOf= and bar validation (#633), and
  =SourcePartition= / =SourceRow=: one provider raw partition with its names, the
  fingerprint of its bytes and, per row, the raw row number, the provider's
  complete flag and an *unvalidated* =Bar= (provider prices, no mid, no
  =AvailableAt=). =Bar.Instrument= (#650) names a bar: readers set it from the
  file's key, it is never a column, and =File.Validate= and
  =SourcePartition.Validate= require it to be empty or the key's symbol. A bare =[]Bar= cannot carry the bad records, row numbers or
  fingerprint this ADR needs, so the hand-off is the partition. It imports =num=
  and the standard library only.
- =provider/oanda=: =ReadSourcePartition= reads the raw partition through the
  store and maps it. It parses and maps only: it derives no mid, sets no
  availability, rejects nothing (a crossed or oddly shaped row is passed through),
  and a row it cannot parse is an error. It never imports =marketdata/service=.
- =marketdata/service= (domain service): =Build=. It classifies every row (the
  reasons of section 3, first match wins: incomplete, misaligned, invalid, crossed,
  then duplicate and out-of-order against the neighbours), sets each good bar's
  mid and =available_at=, fails the build for a row outside the partition's month,
  and publishes. =BuilderVersion= and =ValidatorVersion= live here. It imports
  =marketdata=, =marketdata/internal/canonical=, =num=, =layout=, =store= and the
  standard library; never a provider and never another domain service.
- =marketdata/internal/canonical= (moved there in #635, ADR-091): the
  =canonical-v2= reader and writer, the bad-record type, file validation, the
  alignment and availability rule, and =Publish=, which under the file's lock
  compares the build on disk (=SameBuild=) and writes nothing, builds or replaces.
  It imports =marketdata=, =num=, =layout=, =store= and the standard library; never
  a provider.
- =service/marketdata.go= (application service, package =service=):
  =BuildCanonical= walks the instruments and UTC months of a request, reads each
  raw partition through =provider/oanda=, calls =marketdata/service.Build= and
  reports each partition as built, unchanged, replaced, missing (no raw file for
  the month: reported, not an error) or failed (reported, and the run carries on;
  =ErrPartitionsFailed= at the end). It imports only =marketdata/service= and
  =provider/oanda= and reads no environment. The same package holds the other use
  cases (=Download= and =DownloadRaw=, =LoadSeries=, =VerifyCanonical=, =Audit=,
  =RunBacktest=, =MigrateLayout=); the backtest imports the engine, and only
  =migrate.go= imports =store= and =layout= directly, since a directory migration is
  not market data. A package per use case waits until the files are not small.
- =cmd/trader=: =trader data build=, flags as =download= (=--data-root=,
  =--instrument=, =--interval=, =--from=, =--to=; the same root precedence and
  config file). It needs no token and makes no network call. It prints, per
  partition, built, unchanged, replaced, missing or failed with the good and bad
  counts, then a total. It imports only the application service, the store (for
  =SetRoot=) and logging; no file of =cmd/trader= imports a provider. =download= goes
  through =service.Download=, which builds the OANDA client from a base URL, a request
  gap and a credential the command hands it (#641).
- Scope: =h1= and =d1=. =m1= and =h4= wait until a strategy needs them.
- No Manager, plan, registry or provider interface.
- Architecture tests: only =service/= imports =marketdata/service=; the provider
  never does; the application service imports only the engine, the public model, the
  domain service and a provider (=migrate.go= also the store and the layout); no
  =cmd/trader= file imports a provider, and =build.go=, =status.go=, =verify.go=,
  =audit.go= and =migrate.go= import only the service, the store and logging.

** 6. Tests and the owner's check

Hermetic: raw fixtures are small files copied from the archive; tests never read
=/srv/data=. Cases: each rejection rule written as a bad line and read back; a file
with good and bad rows; a damaged raw row failing the build; a DST =d1= week and the
New York changes; a month boundary; =MidOf= exact on half-pip values, mid required and consistent when bid and ask
are present, single-series bars; an unchanged
rebuild; a rebuild after a changed fingerprint, builder version, validator version
or availability policy; =raw_row= numbering; the reader returning the bad records;
cancellation. A cross-check against the archive:
building a month from its raw fixture must give bid prices and volume equal to the
v1 file's open to close and ticks, and spreads (=Ask - Bid=) whose mean and maximum
equal the v1 file's =avg_spread= and =max_spread=. The owner's check: download a
short range with =trader data download= into a temporary root, run =trader data
build= over it, and read the output.

** 7. Gaps and the market calendar (#534)

The loader checks the interior of a series. Between its first and last bar every
bar the FX calendar expects must be present (=canonical.ClosureAt=, =Gaps=; one
pure function, still no =Calendar= interface):

- The *weekly close* (Friday 17:00 to Sunday 17:00 New York; for =d1= the bars that
  would open on a Friday or Saturday) is not a gap and is never reported.
- A *year-end holiday* gap is reported as a known closure and is never an error.
  The windows are deliberately loose, December 24 12:00 to December 26 17:59 and
  December 31 12:00 to January 2 17:59 New York time, because the archive's closures
  start as early as 14:00 on the 24th or 31st and end as late as 07:00 on the 26th
  or 1st, and a Sunday holiday delays the reopening by a day. Checked against the
  2005 to 2026 archive: every year-end gap lies in a window and nothing else from
  2013 on is a holiday.
- A missing bar that is on the *reviewed list of known provider holes*
  (=marketdata/internal/canonical/known_holes.csv=, =known-provider-holes-v1=) is
  reported as a known hole and is never an error. The list names exact runs
  (source, instrument, interval, start inclusive, end exclusive) that OANDA does not
  have: each was an unexplained gap in the 2026-11-18 audit and every month that
  contained one was downloaded again, and the candles were still absent (280 runs:
  276 =h1= and 4 =d1=, 12 of the 165 distinct missing hours are missing for every
  pair, 64 for one pair only, so a rule based on "all pairs miss it" would not have
  covered them). The list is embedded and parsed strictly (header, interval, UTC
  times on the boundary, sorted, no overlap), and a test checks that no run overlaps
  a weekend or holiday the calendar already explains. The calendar still wins.
  Changing the list is a reviewed change, and a new list is a new version: add a run
  only after checking the provider really has no candle, never to silence a gap a
  download could fill.
- Any other missing bar is *unexplained*, however short. It fails the load, like a
  missing month, unless the request allows missing data (=AllowMissing=); it is
  still reported. A gap that outgrows a listed run is split: the listed part is a
  known hole and the rest is unexplained. =AllowBad= does not excuse a gap.
- A missing bar before the first or after the last bar of the range is not judged: a
  candle still forming is never stored (section 3), so an absent last bar is not
  proof of a gap. The download logs how many forming candles it declined to store
  and prints the count; nothing is written for them.
- A calendar rule is not part of a canonical file, so changing it needs no rebuild.
  Another provider gets its own rule.

Real EURUSD h1, 2013 to 2025: 23 year-end closures and ten unexplained gaps of one or
two hours (for example 2022-05-12 and 2024-05-20), so a strict load of that range
fails until it is allowed.

** 8. Verification against raw (#535)

=marketdata/service.Verify= and =trader data verify= check a canonical file against
the raw partition it should have been built from, without calling the build's own
code. Every raw row must be the source of exactly one bar or the subject of a bad
record, and every bar and bad record must have its raw row. A bar's bid, ask and
volume must equal its row's /exactly/ (raw and canonical are exact fixed-point
decimals, so no numeric tolerance exists), its mid must be their midpoint, and it
must not come from a candle still forming. A bad record's reason must be true of
its row. The expected differences between raw and canonical are therefore exactly
these bad records (forming, misaligned, duplicate, out of order, crossed, invalid),
and the result counts them by reason instead of failing on them. Also checked: the
file's raw fingerprint is that of the raw bytes now on disk, the builder, validator
and availability policy are in force, and the file is valid canonical-v2 (ascending
times, so no duplicates; boundary alignment; valid OHLC; availability). A month with
no raw or no canonical file is reported, not failed. =--sample N= checks N months
per instrument evenly spaced from the first to the last of the range, each in full;
the default is every month.

Not covered: interval resampling (the tree has no derived bars; h1 and d1 are
downloaded, not derived), providers other than OANDA, and the manifest and coverage
audit of #536.

** 9. The audit (#536)

=service.Audit= and =trader data audit= give the operator one answer for a range by
composing the checks above; they define no rule of their own. Per instrument: every
month's state (loaded, bad, missing, stale; the canonical file's metadata, which is
its manifest, is checked by the stale state and the fingerprint finding), the
interior gaps against the calendar of section 7 (weekends not reported, year-end
holidays as known closures, anything else unexplained) and the raw cross-check of
section 8 (which includes duplicate and out-of-order times and invalid OHLC, since
those make a file unreadable as canonical-v2). =--allow-bad= and =--allow-missing=
mean what they do for =status=; =--sample= limits only the raw cross-check. The
report is machine-readable (=--format json=, one object, also when the audit fails)
or text, and the command exits non-zero when the data is not sound. A month that
has a canonical file and no raw partition is /unverifiable/ and fails the audit
(owner review of #673): the audit does not certify what it cannot check, even though
=verify= reports a missing raw only as information; a month with neither file is the
missing month above. With =--sample= the verdict certifies the raw cross-check only
for the sampled months, and the report says so (=sample=, =raw_checked= and a note in
the text output). There is no
equity session calendar and no separate manifest file in this tree (OANDA is the
only provider and the file metadata is the manifest), so neither has a test; an
equity provider brings its own calendar rule.

* Deferred

Coverage of the ends of a range (a range that starts before the archive does, or
ends after it); disk layout checks; the partition cache; loading
canonical bars into the engine's =bar= and frames (a later stage, with the bar
model); =m1=, =h4=, =W1=; other providers; the one-time migration of =raw/oanda= to
=oanda/raw= (now =trader data migrate=, #642, ADR-092), after which the existing raw
archive is converted by this builder.

* Consequences

- Nothing about prices is lost between raw and canonical, and mid is stored (derived once),
  so the basis can be chosen by the consumer and single-price sources fit.
- The existing =canonical-v1= archive is not reused; it is rebuilt from raw once raw
  is migrated. Raw is complete, so nothing is lost, but the rebuild is a step the
  migration must schedule. (Done on 2026-11-18: the v1 files were not migrated, the
  v2 files were built from raw and audited, and the v1 tree is left for the owner to
  delete.)
- A canonical partition can be partial; the invariant in 3 (no consumer treats
  =bad_count > 0= as complete) is part of this decision, not a later option.
- A bad or incomplete record is visible in the file it affects and in the reader's
  result, not only in a build log.
- Files are larger than v1 (eight prices instead of four plus two spreads).
- Availability policy changes need a rebuild, not a code-only change.

* Owner decisions (2026-11-14)

- New schema with an =available_at= column.
- Write the good rows and record the bad rows in the same file, so bad or incomplete
  records never silently lose that distinction.
- =h1= and =d1= only for now.
- A forming candle is not stored and leaves no trace in raw (option 1); a later
  download adds it once it is complete.
- Mid basis, with open and close preserved: the canonical bar keeps bid and ask
  open, high, low and close and the volume. Mid was first a method; on the review
  of #631 the owner chose a stored field, derived once, so that single-price
  sources can write to the series they are (rules in section 1).
