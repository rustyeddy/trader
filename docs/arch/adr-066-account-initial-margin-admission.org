#+TITLE: ADR-066: Account-level initial-margin admission for the simulator/backtester
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Accepted. Issue #410, the first step of milestone Account Margin
Admission (umbrella #409). Every later issue in the milestone (#411-#417)
implements against the decisions recorded here. No code is introduced by
this ADR.

* Context

A buy-and-hold equity backtest with $10,000 of equity opened 100 SPY
shares at about $463.08 — $46,308 notional, roughly 4.6× equity — on
what was meant to be an unlevered account (#409). Three facts made that
possible:

1. =risk.NewFixedFractionSizer= answers "how much am I willing to lose?"
   (risk budget ÷ adverse distance). Its result is unbounded as the stop
   tightens, and it never consults buying power.
2. The backtest composition root (=cmd/trader/backtest/service.go=)
   builds =risk.NewEngine()= with no rules at all.
3. The simulator (=internal/adapters/broker/sim=) has no margin policy:
   =BuyingPower= and =MarginAvailable= mirror cash, =MarginUsed= is
   always zero, and cash may go negative — a documented M3 placeholder.

The existing =MaxPositionLeverageRule= (#184, ADR-034) is per-position:
every position passing it does not bound the account's aggregate
exposure. Its own doc comment names the missing capability — a current
mark for every open listing — which is also why #183 deferred a
=MaxAggregateExposureRule=.

Fixed-fraction risk sizing and financing are different controls, and
both are needed. This ADR decides the financing control's semantics.
Background vocabulary and the wider roadmap live in
=docs/account-risk.md= (#418); this ADR is authoritative where they
differ.

* Decision

** Terminology and the admission invariant

=initial_margin_ratio= is the minimum equity required per unit of gross
position notional:

| Ratio | Maximum gross exposure |
|-------+------------------------|
|  1.0  | 1× equity (unlevered)  |
|  0.5  | 2× equity              |
|  0.25 | 4× equity              |

The ratio form is kept rather than a =max_leverage= setting, so that
=maintenance_margin_ratio= can be added later with the matching meaning
and without renaming an existing field.

The invariant is an *initial-margin admission* rule, not a continuously
maintained account state:

#+begin_example
prospective gross notional × initial_margin_ratio ≤ equity
#+end_example

It is evaluated only for a proposal or fill that would increase gross
exposure. Existing exposure is never liquidated, and no margin call is
raised, because later price movement pushes the account over the ratio.
While an account is over the limit because of that drift, further
exposure-increasing proposals and fills are rejected, and de-risking
ones are admitted.

** Gross, not net

Gross notional is the sum of the absolute notional of every open
position:

#+begin_example
gross = Σ |quantityᵢ| × priceᵢ × multiplierᵢ
#+end_example

Longs and shorts both add. Nothing is netted across long and short
positions or across instruments.

** Resulting position, not position plus order

The proposal's own instrument contributes its *resulting* position, as
computed by the existing =risk.resultingPosition= semantics (including
=ReduceOnly= clamping). A 100-long → 100-short reversal contributes 100
short, never 200 or 300 units.

#+begin_example
prospective gross = gross of every other open position
                  + |resulting quantity| × price × multiplier   (proposal's instrument)
#+end_example

** De-risking exemption

A proposal whose prospective gross notional does not exceed the current
gross notional — both computed on the same valuation basis, below — is
always admitted, even when the account is already over the limit. With
every other position held constant, this reduces to ADR-034's own
magnitude test: the resulting position's magnitude in the proposal's
instrument does not exceed the current one's. The account-level rule
therefore applies ADR-034 without a separate definition of
"de-risking".

This exemption is part of the initial-margin rule's own contract, like
the other position/exposure-limit rules ADR-034 covers. It is not a
requirement on every future risk rule.

** Valuation basis

Two prices are involved, and they are never blended for one position:

- *The proposal's instrument* is valued, both before and after the
  proposal, at =risk.Input.ReferencePrice= at admission and at the actual
  fill price (after slippage and tick rounding) at fill time. Using the
  same price for "before" and "after" keeps the de-risking comparison a
  pure magnitude comparison.
- *Every other open position* is valued at its current mark.

A position's historical =AvgPrice= is never used for valuation (#183's
valuation-basis rule).

Current marks are exposed per open position on =account.Snapshot=
(#412), each carrying the price and the time it was observed. The
simulator already holds these internally (=accountState.marks=, updated
by fills, =ObserveMark=, and =AdvanceBar=). The mark is "as of the
simulator's last observation of that listing", not a live price.

Staleness is defined, not eliminated:

- Admission during =Scheduler= Phase 2 (flushing intents at time T)
  sees marks from the previous batch's closes. Fill-time checks for
  resting orders run in Phase 3, after the same batch's
  =ObserveMark= calls. This is the scheduler's existing no-lookahead
  ordering, and this ADR does not change it.
- In a multi-instrument run whose instruments don't share a calendar,
  one instrument's mark can be older than another's. Each is still the
  latest observation at or before the snapshot's =AsOf=, which is the
  best deterministic value available. Mark times are exposed so a
  report can show them.
- An open position with no mark is a classifiable
  =risk.ErrInsufficientRuleInput= error, never valued at zero or at
  =AvgPrice=. (In the simulator every position gets a mark from its
  opening fill, so this indicates a bug or an adapter without marks.)

** One calculation, two enforcement points

The gross-notional and required-margin arithmetic lives in one small,
pure, exact-numeric (ADR-004) package that both =internal/risk= and
=internal/adapters/broker/sim= import (#411). =sim= does not import
=risk=. Each enforcement point supplies its own inputs:

| Enforcement point           | Proposal's instrument | Other positions     | Equity                                 |
|-----------------------------+-----------------------+---------------------+----------------------------------------|
| Admission (risk rule, #413) | =ReferencePrice=      | snapshot marks      | snapshot =Equity()=                    |
| Fill time (simulator, #415) | actual fill price     | simulator marks     | equity after this fill's own commission |

Admission cannot see the commission model, so it compares against
pre-fee equity. The simulator knows the exact commission of the fill it
is about to book, so the fill-time check uses equity after that
commission. The fill-time check is therefore never less conservative
than admission. At exactly the limit, an order admitted before fees can
be refused at fill because of its own commission. That is deliberate:
the invariant is about what the account actually holds after the fill.

The calculation is defined per position — Σ notionalᵢ × rateᵢ — and v1
supplies the single account-level ratio as every position's rate. A
later per-listing or per-instrument margin policy (OANDA's per-instrument
=marginRate=, or futures' fixed dollars per contract) changes only that
per-position input, not the aggregate calculation or the meaning of the
account-level setting. v1 designs for this but does not implement it or
test mixed rates.

** The account-level risk rule

A new =risk.Rule=, named =account_initial_margin= (#413), distinct from
and in addition to =MaxPositionLeverageRule=:

- Strict approve/reject (ADR-029). It never changes a proposal's
  quantity, and nothing else in Trader resizes a rejected order.
- A violation's =Measured= is the prospective required margin and its
  =Limit= is equity. Its =Message= includes current gross notional,
  prospective gross notional, and the ratio, so the journal shows why
  the proposal was rejected.
- A missing =ReferencePrice= or mark, a non-positive ratio, or a
  currency mismatch is a classifiable error (=ErrInsufficientRuleInput=
  or =ErrInvalidRule=), never an approval.

** Fill-time enforcement in the simulator

The simulator re-checks every exposure-increasing fill at the actual
fill price (#415). It is configured as an ADR-028-style model on the
simulated account, so its =ModelInfo= (name, version, canonical
config, including the ratio) reaches the run manifest and
=ConfigDigest=. A simulated account with no margin model configured
keeps today's behavior, and its =ModelInfo= says so explicitly. This
keeps existing =sim= unit tests meaningful; the backtest composition
root always configures one (#414).

An over-limit fill is not booked, in whole. There is no partial fill
down to the limit and no resizing. Reduce-only and de-risking fills are
never blocked.

*** Order lifecycle: no ADR-018 amendment

ADR-018 permits =StatusRejected= only from =StatusPendingSubmit=, and
there is no direct =StatusWorking= → =StatusCanceled= edge. Both cases
fit the existing graph:

- *Market order refused inside =Submit=.* Nothing has been accepted
  yet, so the simulator returns the order as =StatusRejected= with
  =Rejection{Reason: ReasonInsufficientMargin, Detail: <numbers>}= —
  the same pre-acceptance rejection a real broker gives, and the
  existing reason code for it. No accept event is emitted.
- *Resting limit/stop order refused when it triggers* (Phase 3,
  =AdvanceBar=). The order is already =StatusWorking=, so it is
  retired by the simulator's existing broker-initiated cancellation
  path, =buildInternalCancellation= — =StatusWorking= →
  =StatusPendingCancel= → =StatusCanceled=, the same two-event
  sequence #352 already uses for a stale reduce-only order. This is
  also how OANDA handles an order that triggers without enough margin
  (canceled with an insufficient-margin reason).

The one thing missing is a way to say *why* a broker-initiated cancel
happened: =order.Order.Rejection= is, by invariant, non-nil only on a
=StatusRejected= order. This ADR adds an optional
=Order.CancelReason *Rejection=, reusing the =Rejection= type. It is
non-nil only on a =StatusCanceled= order that the broker canceled on its
own initiative, and nil for an operator- or strategy-requested cancel.
=order.NewOrder= enforces that invariant. This is an additive change to
the internal order vocabulary (ADR-017). It adds no transition, so
ADR-018's graph is unchanged. The #352 reduce-only cancellation may
adopt the field later; that is not required here.

*** Bracket entries (ADR-059)

Today, if a bracket's entry leg does not fill synchronously, the
pipeline returns =ErrBracketEntryNotSynchronouslyFilled=. The scheduler
treats only a /risk/ rejection of an unfilled entry as harmless; any
other error aborts the run. A simulator margin rejection of the entry
leg is the same harmless case — nothing was opened and the stop leg was
never attempted — so it must not abort the run. #415 makes a broker
rejection of an unfilled entry leg classifiable, and the scheduler
treats it exactly like an entry-side risk rejection: the run continues.
Once the entry has filled, the existing rule stands: any stop-leg failure
aborts the run. The protective stop itself is reduce-only and never
counts toward prospective exposure.

** Open orders

Resting, non-reduce-only orders do not count toward prospective
exposure at admission in v1. In the simulator, the fill-time check
catches the case where several resting entries each pass admission and
then fill together. Reduce-only orders, including ADR-059 protective
stops, never count.

This is *not* sufficient for a live broker. There, the broker's own
margin engine, or a Trader-side buying-power reservation made on
approval and released on cancel or reject, decides. Pending-order
reservation is deferred and must be revisited before any live trading
(M6/M8). Admission makes no claim to cover it.

** Ratio 1.0 means "unlevered", not "cash account"

=initial_margin_ratio: 1.0= caps gross exposure at equity, and permits
short positions up to that cap. A real cash account cannot short at all.
v1 does not model that restriction. A future, separate, explicit
setting (for example =allow_short=) would be recorded in the manifest
and enforced by its own rule, never implied by the ratio. Until then,
reports and docs describe ratio 1.0 as an "unlevered margin account".

** Scope of the setting

=backtest.initial_margin_ratio= configures the simulated account and the
backtest risk engine together, from one config value (#414). The value
must be positive. It is not a Trader-wide or live setting: a real broker
adapter reports its own margin state, and live admission against it is
future work.

** Currency

v1 supports one account currency and instruments that settle in it.
There is no FX conversion inside the calculation. A position or proposal
whose settlement currency differs from the account currency is a
classifiable error, matching the existing money-valuing rules and
sizers.

** Default and compatibility

The default is =1.0=. Any existing backtest that relied on implicit
leverage produces different results: orders that previously filled over
equity are now rejected. This includes golden fixtures (the ADR-041
determinism suite, =report= goldens, =sma_long_hold_equivalence_test=).
#414 regenerates them through their documented procedures and lists
every changed fixture and why. A run can reproduce the old behavior
explicitly with a smaller ratio, for example =0.25=.

** Non-goals (v1)

- Maintenance margin, margin calls, forced liquidation, closeout
  simulation.
- Broker-specific Reg-T or portfolio-margin replication.
- Cross-currency margin.
- Per-instrument margin rates (designed for, not implemented).
- Shorting restrictions for cash accounts.
- Pending-order buying-power reservation.
- Any silent quantity resizing, by a strategy or by Trader.

* Consequences

- Backtests can no longer hold exposure the account couldn't finance,
  and every refusal is observable, either as a risk decision or as a
  broker event with a reason.
- Account snapshots gain per-position marks (#412). This is the
  capability #183 and #184 deferred, and it unblocks other account-wide
  rules later (for example aggregate exposure or portfolio heat).
- The simulator's =BuyingPower=, =MarginUsed=, and =MarginAvailable=
  become real, derived values when a margin model is configured (#412),
  so =FullNotionalSizer= (ADR-061), which reads =BuyingPower=, respects
  the ratio.
- =order.Order= gains =CancelReason=. Anything that renders or
  journals orders should show it.
- The bracket-entry path gains one more harmless outcome (a broker
  margin rejection of an unfilled entry). Its safety rule for a filled
  entry is unchanged.
- Strategies that choose their own quantity (Buy & Hold) keep that
  contract. A quantity the account can't finance is rejected, and
  choosing a financeable quantity is configuration guidance (#417), not
  a sizer change.
- Existing leveraged results change under the 1.0 default. This is
  intended and must be called out wherever fixtures move.

* Alternatives Considered

- *A =max_leverage= setting.* Rejected. It reads naturally for one
  number but has no counterpart for maintenance margin, and
  "margin ratio" is how venues describe the control.
- *Net exposure.* Rejected. A long/short book with little net exposure
  can still need substantial margin; gross is the conservative and
  conventional basis.
- *Resize to fit.* Rejected (ADR-029, and the #409 decision). A
  quantity Trader silently shrank is not the trade the strategy asked
  for, and it hides the reason in the results.
- *Value other positions at =AvgPrice=.* Rejected (#183). It mixes a
  historical cost basis with current prices and understates exposure
  after gains.
- *Admission-only, with no fill-time check.* Rejected. Next-bar fills,
  gaps, slippage, and resting orders that trigger together can all
  create an over-limit position that admission never saw.
- *Continuous enforcement, with liquidation on breach.* Rejected for v1.
  That is maintenance-margin semantics, which needs its own policy for
  what gets closed, when, and at what price.
- *Amend ADR-018 to allow =StatusWorking= → =StatusRejected=.* Rejected.
  Rejection is a pre-acceptance concept in ADR-018 and at real brokers.
  A broker-initiated cancel with a recorded reason fits the existing
  graph and matches how brokers behave.
- *Carry the cancel reason only on the broker event.* Rejected. The
  order's own state would not explain its terminal status, and every
  consumer of orders (journal, reports, strategies' fill feedback)
  would have to correlate events to find out.
- *Put the ratio in the risk engine only.* Rejected. It leaves the
  simulator able to book an over-limit fill, and it gives the admission
  and fill checks two separately configured values that could drift
  apart.
