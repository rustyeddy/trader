#+TITLE: ADR-095: Trade-price canonical bars and US equity daily semantics
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Proposed. Issue #733 (Equities-01). Nothing here is implemented, and no code or
data changes before the owner accepts it. On acceptance it amends ADR-090 (which
deferred a persisted trade series to "a separate ADR and schema decision") and
ADR-088 (which values currency pairs only). ADR-073's finding that Stooq daily
history is dividend-adjusted is carried over. ADR-047 to ADR-049 describe archived
code and are inputs, not constraints. (ADR-094 was withdrawn; its number is not
reused.)

* Context

The goal is one honest backtest of SPY daily bars from Stooq
(=docs/research/equity-data-inventory.md=, #728).

- =marketdata.Bar= has a =Trade= series, but =canonical-v2= refuses it and has no
  column for it. Writing trade prices to =Mid= would label them as a midpoint.
- D1 alignment and availability are FX: open and close at 17:00 New York. The gap
  calendar is FX.
- Canonical metadata does not say what a price is, in which currency, or how it
  was adjusted. Stooq SPY is dividend-adjusted: every ex-dividend date rewrites
  all earlier prices.
- The engine values currency pairs only, rounds bars to a tick and puts the
  result in =Mid=.

Checked read-only on 2026-11-25 in =d_us_txt.zip=
(sha256 =962aec12a900c9debefbdc1c6f9cffb9dc109a4eaa8aafc8cbf106d97382a52c=):

- Across 27,890,673 daily rows no price has more than 8 decimals, the scale of
  =num.Price= (ADR-004). Five members hold prices above its maximum of
  92,233,720,368.54775807 and cannot be represented. SPY has at most 4 decimals
  and only whole volumes.
- SPY has 5,418 rows from 2005-02-25 to 2026-09-10 and 202 weekdays without one.
  Rule-derived NYSE holidays plus five unscheduled closures (2007-01-02,
  2012-10-29, 2012-10-30, 2018-12-05, 2025-01-09) explain 201. *The other,
  2011-02-17, looks like a hole in the data:* QQQ, AAPL, MSFT, JPM and XOM have
  that day; SPY, IWM, DIA and GLD do not. This corrects the inventory's reading
  that the gaps were consistent with closures. The holiday rules were not checked
  against an exchange publication.

* Decision

** 1. A second schema, =canonical-trade-v1=

Trade-only data gets its own schema. =canonical-v2= is not changed.

| Source | Schema             | Price series  |
|--------+--------------------+---------------|
| oanda  | canonical-v2       | bid, ask, mid |
| stooq  | canonical-trade-v1 | trade         |

The source fixes the schema; a file whose schema does not match its source is
malformed. A =canonical-trade-v1= bar has =Trade= set and =Bid=, =Ask= and =Mid=
absent. No side, midpoint or spread is derived. The path layout is that of
ADR-089: =stooq/canonical/SPY/2024/03/SPY-2024-03-d1.csv=. Bad records, atomic
publication and rebuild rules are those of ADR-090. The only interval is =d1=.

** 2. Prices and volume are exact

- Prices are written with exactly 8 decimals, the full scale of =num.Price=. This
  covers SPY's 4, AAPL's 7 and every value in the bundle without a per-symbol
  width. A price with more precision or out of range is a bad record (=invalid=),
  never rounded.
- Volume is a whole number of shares (=volume_unit=). A fractional volume (some
  early Stooq rows, such as IWM 2005) is a bad record in this version. Accepting
  it is a later decision, needed before those symbols, not before SPY.
- =Bar.Volume='s meaning therefore depends on the source: tick count for OANDA,
  shares here. Its documentation changes to say so.

** 3. Adjustment and provenance

All of the new metadata fields are required, take part in =SameBuild=, and are set
by the build, never inferred by a reader.

| Field                  | Meaning                                                        |
|------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| =price_basis=          | =trade=                                                        |
| =currency=             | quote currency, =USD=                                          |
| =volume_unit=          | =shares=                                                       |
| =adjustment=           | =total-return=, =split=, =unadjusted= or =unknown=             |
| =adjustment_evidence=  | =observed= (inferred by comparing data) or =documented=        |
| =snapshot_fingerprint= | sha256 of the one whole-history raw file the series came from  |
| =snapshot_as_of=       | date that file was obtained, declared by the operator          |
| =raw_fingerprint=      | as ADR-090: the raw rows of this month                         |

- Stooq D1 is =total-return= / =observed= (ADR-073, #728).
- *A series is one snapshot.* Because adjustment rewrites history, months from two
  downloads must never be joined. A load whose months differ in schema, currency,
  adjustment or =snapshot_fingerprint= fails, and no flag allows it. A new
  download rebuilds the whole series.
- The Stooq build therefore reads one whole-history file (the bundle member or a
  loose download), not independently updatable monthly files. A snapshot a run
  used is kept, so the run can be reproduced. The provider issue fixes the path.
- Alpaca (split-adjusted by its levels, setting unrecorded, IEX volume) is a
  different source and series. Nothing merges or splices sources.
- The loader returns these fields with the bars. =run.json= and the REST bars
  response gain an additive =data= object with them and the availability policy.
- *Unknown, and recorded as such rather than filled:* dividend and split events and
  amounts, the vendor's adjustment method, whether open and close are auction
  prices, the volume scope beyond "consolidated by observation", and restatements
  between downloads.

** 4. Daily time, availability and calendar

Policy =us-equity-close-v1=, for the exchange date =D= of a row, in
America/New_York using the zone bytes already pinned in the repository:

- =Time= is 09:30 on =D=, the scheduled regular open.
- =AvailableAt= is 16:00 on =D=, or 13:00 when =D= is a listed early close. It is
  modeled, not observed, as in ADR-090. It is never earlier than the scheduled
  close: an early close that is not listed only delays availability to 16:00.
- Unscheduled late openings or intraday halts are not modeled; =Time= stays
  nominal.

The calendar is a reviewed, embedded table of full closures and early closes,
=us-equity-calendar-v1=, covering 2005-01-01 to 2026-12-31. It is a list of dates,
not computed rules and not a =Calendar= interface. An early close is listed only
when an exchange publication confirms it. A date outside the table fails the
build. Because the table decides =AvailableAt=, changing it is a new policy name
and a rebuild.

- A row dated on a closed day is a bad record with a new reason, =non-session=.
- A trading date with no row is a gap. It fails a strict load unless it is on a
  reviewed known-holes list, as in ADR-090 section 7. SPY 2011-02-17 is proposed
  for that list, after one fresh download confirms Stooq still lacks it.

Required test fixtures:

| 2024-03-08 | last day before DST          | 14:30        | 21:00               |
| 2024-03-11 | first day of DST             | 13:30        | 20:00               |

| 2024-11-04 | first day after DST          | 14:30        | 21:00               |
| 2024-11-27 | normal day                   | 14:30        | 21:00               |

Plus: a row on a closed day, a date outside the table, and a bar refused to a
consumer one instant before =AvailableAt=.

** 5. First-test execution and scope

Dividend-adjusted prices are accepted for a first backtest *to verify the pipeline
and to study returns*. They are not evidence of executable performance:

- Past prices are lower than what traded, so share counts, notionals and any
  price-level rule are not historical. Percentage moves are approximately
  preserved.
- There is no bid, ask or spread. No commission, spread or slippage is charged.
  Results are optimistic by those costs, and =run.json= says =costs: none=.
- Dividends are already in the prices; the engine credits none.

Engine rules for this source, applied only to runs on it:

| Topic       | Rule                                                                 |
|-------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Instrument  | SPY as an ETF settling in USD, multiplier 1                          |
| Basis       | the engine's bars keep the series as =Trade=, never relabelled =Mid= |
| Tick        | 0.00000001, so adjusted prices are never rounded                     |
| Size        | whole shares, minimum 1                                              |
| Leverage    | margin rate 1 (cash only); risk per trade unchanged (ADR-093)        |
| Short       | an opening short is rejected: no borrow, locate or dividend model    |
| Fills       | existing rules: a decision at the close fills no earlier than the next bar's open; a gap through a stop fills at the open; the intrabar assumption of the broker applies |

Allowed first scope: one instrument, D1, 2005-02-25 to 2026-09-10, one fixed
long-only strategy with market entries and stops set as a percentage distance. No
dollar thresholds, no parameter optimisation, no multi-instrument or FX mix.

* Compatibility and migration

- =canonical-v2= files, =scheduled-close-v1=, the FX calendar, the known FX holes,
  OANDA builder and validator versions and the default source stay as they are. No
  file is migrated or rebuilt.
- New: the =canonical-trade-v1= codec, the =non-session= reason (valid only in it),
  the equity availability policy and calendar, and =stooq= accepted by the build,
  read and backtest paths.
- The SPY market is added to the catalog only for =stooq= runs, so the settings
  recorded for an FX run do not change.
- The JSON additions are additive; existing fields keep their meaning.
- Each implementation step keeps the existing FX tests and the EMAX scenario
  checksum unchanged, and adds a test that an OANDA file encodes byte for byte as
  before.

* Consequences

- Two codecs share the file rules; the shared parts are factored, not copied.
- A backtest range on Stooq is all-or-nothing on one snapshot, and old snapshots
  accumulate.
- 8-decimal columns are wider than SPY needs.
- Symbols with fractional volume or out-of-range prices are not fully loadable yet.
- Follow-up issues, in order: schema and calendar; =provider/stooq= and the SPY
  build; engine market rules; the first backtest with golden output.

* Alternatives considered

- *Add trade columns to =canonical-v2=, or one new superset schema.* Rejected: the
  strict reader would force every FX file to be rebuilt or two column sets under
  one name, to store columns that are always empty.
- *Write trade prices to =Mid=.* Rejected: it mislabels data (#733).
- *Decimals per instrument.* Rejected: another property to maintain and a blocker
  at expansion, for a few bytes saved.
- *Midnight UTC daily bars (ADR-049).* Rejected: =Time= would precede the session
  by hours and say nothing about when the bar may be used.
- *Always make a bar available at 16:00 or later, with no early-close list.* Safe
  and simpler, but #733 asks for early closes to be handled.
- *Computed holiday rules.* Rejected: unscheduled closures need a list anyway.
- *Wait for unadjusted prices with dividend events before any backtest.* Better
  evidence, but no such data is on hand. It stays the requirement for a result
  meant to support trading.

* Decisions for the owner

1. Separate =canonical-trade-v1= schema, leaving =canonical-v2= untouched?
2. 8-decimal prices, and fractional volume rejected for now?
3. One snapshot per series, with no override?
4. =Time= 09:30, =AvailableAt= scheduled close, from an embedded date table?
5. SPY 2011-02-17 as a known hole (recommended), or leave the load failing unless
   missing data is allowed?
6. Dividend-adjusted prices acceptable for the first test under the limits above?
7. Reject opening shorts for equities (recommended), or allow them with the result
   marked as ignoring borrow costs?
