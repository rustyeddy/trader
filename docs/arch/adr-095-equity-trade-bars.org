#+TITLE: ADR-095: Trade-price canonical bars and US equity daily semantics
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Status

Accepted (owner approval 2026-11-26, #733). Nothing here is implemented yet. The
calendar verification and 2011-02-17 conditions in section 4 still apply. It
amends ADR-090 (which
deferred a persisted trade series to "a separate ADR and schema decision") and
ADR-088 (which values currency pairs only). ADR-073's finding that Stooq daily
history is dividend-adjusted is carried over. ADR-047 to ADR-049 describe archived
code and are inputs, not constraints. (ADR-094 was withdrawn; its number is not
reused.)

* Context

The goal is one honest backtest of SPY daily bars from Stooq
(=docs/research/equity-data-inventory.md=, #728).

- =marketdata.Bar= has a =Trade= series, but =canonical-v2= refuses it and has no
  column for it. Writing trade prices to =Mid= would label them as a midpoint.
- D1 alignment and availability are FX: open and close at 17:00 New York. The gap
  calendar is FX.
- Canonical metadata does not say what a price is, in which currency, or how it
  was adjusted. Stooq SPY is dividend-adjusted: every ex-dividend date rewrites
  all earlier prices.
- The engine values currency pairs only, rounds bars to a tick and puts the
  result in =Mid=.

Checked read-only on 2026-11-25 in =d_us_txt.zip=
(sha256 =962aec12a900c9debefbdc1c6f9cffb9dc109a4eaa8aafc8cbf106d97382a52c=):

- Across 27,890,673 daily rows no price has more than 8 decimals, the scale of
  =num.Price= (ADR-004). Five members hold prices above its maximum of
  92,233,720,368.54775807 and cannot be represented. SPY has at most 4 decimals
  and only whole volumes.
- SPY has 5,418 rows from 2005-02-25 to 2026-09-10 and 202 weekdays without one.
  Rule-derived NYSE holidays plus five unscheduled closures (2007-01-02,
  2012-10-29, 2012-10-30, 2018-12-05, 2025-01-09) explain 201. *The other,
  2011-02-17, looks like a hole in the data:* QQQ, AAPL, MSFT, JPM and XOM have
  that day; SPY, IWM, DIA and GLD do not. This corrects the inventory's reading
  that the gaps were consistent with closures. The holiday rules were not checked
  against an exchange publication.

* Universal bar model and consumer contract

Trader has *one* domain bar, =marketdata.Bar=, regardless of source, asset class,
storage schema or consumer. The existing =Bid=, =Ask=, =Mid= and =Trade= OHLC
series are optional observations of the *same* bar. What a provider supplies
determines which series are present; it does not determine the Go bar type.

=Bar.OHLC()= already solves the common strategy/indicator/study case. It returns
the first available series in the existing, stable priority order:

  =Mid -> Trade -> Bid -> Ask=

General-purpose strategies, indicators, scanners and studies use =OHLC()=
without branching on a provider or market. Consumers which actually need
quotes, such as spread-sensitive research and execution logic, explicitly read
=Bid= and =Ask= and check whether they exist. They *must not* infer a missing
quote, spread, midpoint or executable price from a trade-only or unknown-basis
series. Consumers must honor =AvailableAt= and understand that the preferred
series is for price analysis, not necessarily for executable fills.

There is no separate equity bar, FX bar, strategy bar, or modified
consumer-specific Bar. The tempting alternative of transforming a =Bar= into a
different type for each consumer is *rejected*: it moves the same semantic
questions into conversion code, creates extra models and potential information
loss, and does not remove consumers' responsibility to understand the data they
use. The public contract must instead document preferred-price selection,
presence/absence, availability, source price basis, and execution limitations.
A specialized derived view may be an explicit algorithmic choice, never a new
required canonical representation or a silent conversion.

Currently an absent OHLC series is the all-zero value, detected by
=OHLC.IsZero()=. This is already unambiguous for validated bars (a present
OHLC must have positive prices). The idea of making series =*OHLC= pointers
and/or private fields accessed via getters is *deferred*. It requires an API
migration and performance analysis: pointers can reduce inline size but can
introduce allocations, garbage-collector work and indirection. Benchmark real
single- and multi-instrument histories and profile memory/throughput first.
Neither change is a prerequisite for SPY D1. Preserve the current =OHLC()=
signature and priority behavior in this milestone.

Persistence may use versioned encodings for different available series, but
*every* reader must reconstruct the identical =marketdata.Bar= model and
presence semantics, without a per-consumer adaptation layer. The schema is
identified and validated from the file's declared columns/capabilities and
metadata, *not inherently from the provider's name*. A provider may supply
trade-only bars, quote bars or both at different times. Preserve every
available price series in canonical storage. Dataset-level provenance records
the source, declared/observed price basis, adjustment and snapshot, while
=OHLC()= selects the preferred populated series within an individual bar.

* Decision

** 1. A second on-disk schema, =canonical-trade-v1=

For the initial implementation, trade-only data gets an additional *on-disk
encoding*, not an additional domain bar or consumer API. Existing
=canonical-v2= FX files are unchanged. The target layouts for today's known
datasets are:

| Observations in file | On-disk schema       | Example source |
|----------------------+----------------------+----------------|
| bid, ask, mid        | canonical-v2         | OANDA          |
| trade                | canonical-trade-v1   | Stooq, Alpaca  |

The *declared schema and observed columns* must agree; the source is provenance,
not a permanent schema selector. A future source may provide different series
without changing the =marketdata.Bar= API. The choice of two on-disk codecs is
a narrow compatibility decision, not an assertion that different markets need
different canonical data models. A later common superset encoding can be
evaluated separately without forcing an FX migration in this milestone.

The build chooses the encoding from the series observed in its input. A reader
dispatches on the =schema= in the header and rejects columns that do not match
it. Neither encoding holds quotes and trade prices together, so a build whose
input has both fails: it never drops a series to fit a file. Such data needs a
new encoding first. Which parser and market rules apply to =stooq= is
configuration for this scope, not a rule that a provider has one schema.

A =canonical-trade-v1= reader returns a normal =marketdata.Bar= with =Trade=
present and =Bid=, =Ask=, =Mid= absent. =Bar.OHLC()= consequently chooses =Trade=
without changing any strategy or indicator. No side, midpoint or spread is
derived. The path layout is that of
ADR-089: =stooq/canonical/SPY/2024/03/SPY-2024-03-d1.csv=. Bad records, atomic
publication and rebuild rules are those of ADR-090. The only interval is =d1=.

** 2. Prices and volume are exact

- Prices are written with exactly 8 decimals, the full scale of =num.Price=. This
  covers SPY's 4, AAPL's 7 and every value in the bundle without a per-symbol
  width. A price with more precision or out of range is a bad record (=invalid=),
  never rounded.
- Volume is a whole number of shares (=volume_unit=). A fractional volume (some
  early Stooq rows, such as IWM 2005) is a bad record in this version. Accepting
  it is a later decision, needed before those symbols, not before SPY.
- =Bar.Volume='s meaning therefore depends on the source: tick count for OANDA,
  shares here. Its documentation changes to say so.

** 3. Adjustment and provenance

All of the new metadata fields are required, take part in =SameBuild=, and are set
by the build, never inferred by a reader.

| Field                  | Meaning                                                        |
|------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| =price_basis=          | =trade=                                                        |
| =currency=             | quote currency, =USD=                                          |
| =volume_unit=          | =shares=                                                       |
| =adjustment=           | =total-return=, =split=, =unadjusted= or =unknown=             |
| =adjustment_evidence=  | =observed= (inferred by comparing data) or =documented=        |
| =snapshot_fingerprint= | sha256 of the series' whole-history input bytes (below)        |
| =snapshot_as_of=       | date those bytes were obtained, declared by the operator       |
| =calendar=             | version of the date table used (section 4)                     |
| =raw_fingerprint=      | as ADR-090: the raw rows of this month                         |

- Stooq D1 is =total-return= / =observed= (ADR-073, #728).
- *A series is one snapshot.* Because adjustment rewrites history, months from two
  downloads must never be joined. A load whose months differ in schema, currency,
  adjustment, calendar or =snapshot_fingerprint= fails, and no flag
  allows it. A new download rebuilds the whole series.
- The Stooq build therefore reads one whole-history file for one symbol, not
  independently updatable monthly files.
- *Input bytes.* =snapshot_fingerprint= is the sha256 of exactly that file's
  bytes: the uncompressed bytes of the symbol's ZIP member, or the bytes of a
  loose download. Line endings and encoding are not normalized. The ZIP's own
  name, hash, timestamps and compression are not part of the identity, so
  repackaging a bundle does not change it. A loose download is the same snapshot
  only if it is byte-identical. The loose =*_us_d.csv= format differs from the
  member format, so it is a different snapshot even where every price agrees.
  For the bundle inspected here, the SPY member
  (=data/daily/us/nyse etfs/2/spy.us.txt=) is
  =54b64bba890d89495bc2108824ba378a3f7f92349d5021595499bbc47696ee6b=.
- *Archive.* Before publishing canonical files, the build stores those bytes
  unchanged at =stooq/snapshots/SPY/<sha256>.raw= under the data root, with
  =<sha256>.json= beside it recording =snapshot_as_of=, the original file name
  and, for a member, the member path and the ZIP's sha256. Neither file is
  overwritten: a later build of the same bytes verifies them and reuses the
  recorded =snapshot_as_of=. The archived bytes, builder version and calendar
  are enough to rebuild the series.
- Alpaca (split-adjusted by its levels, setting unrecorded, IEX volume) is a
  different source and series. Nothing merges or splices sources.
- The loader returns these fields alongside the *unchanged*
  =marketdata.Bar= values. =run.json= and the REST bars response gain an
  additive =data= object with them and the availability policy. Record the
  selected price basis and data limitations so a general =OHLC()= consumer's
  research output can be interpreted correctly.
- *Unknown, and recorded as such rather than filled:* dividend and split events and
  amounts, the vendor's adjustment method, whether open and close are auction
  prices, the volume scope beyond "consolidated by observation", and restatements
  between downloads.

** 4. Daily time, availability and calendar

Policy =us-equity-close-v1=, for the exchange date =D= of a row, in
America/New_York using the zone bytes already pinned in the repository:

- =Time= is 09:30 on =D=, the scheduled regular open.
- =AvailableAt= is 16:00 on =D=, or 13:00 when =D= is a listed early close. It is
  modeled, not observed, as in ADR-090. It is never earlier than the scheduled
  close: an early close that is not listed only delays availability to 16:00.
- Unscheduled late openings or intraday halts are not modeled; =Time= stays
  nominal.

The calendar is a reviewed, embedded table of full closures and early closes,
=us-equity-calendar-v1=, covering 2005-01-01 to 2026-12-31. It is a list of dates,
not computed rules and not a =Calendar= interface. A date outside the table fails
the build.

- *Source.* The New York Stock Exchange's published holiday and trading-hours
  calendars, and its notices for unscheduled closures. Each entry, or each year's
  group of entries, cites the publication it was checked against.
- *Not yet verified.* The dates discussed here come from holiday rules and the
  Stooq rows. The table is not authoritative until every full closure and early
  close from 2005 to 2026 has been checked, and the issue that adds it is not
  merged before then. An early close without a confirming publication is not
  listed.
- *Updates.* A version is never edited after data has been built with it. Any
  change to the dates, whether a correction or another year, is a new version,
  reviewed with its citations, and series are rebuilt to use it.
- *Pinning.* Each file records =calendar= beside =availability_policy=, both are
  part of =SameBuild=, and =run.json= records both. The policy name changes only
  when the timing rules change; the calendar version changes with the dates.

- A row dated on a closed day is a bad record with a new reason, =non-session=.
- A trading date with no row is a gap. It fails a strict load unless it is on a
  reviewed known-holes list, as in ADR-090 section 7.
- SPY 2011-02-17 is *not* on that list. It is added only after an authoritative
  record confirms SPY traded that day and a fresh Stooq download still lacks it.
  Until then a strict load covering that day fails, which includes the first
  test's range. If a fresh download has the row, the new snapshot is used and no
  exemption is needed.

** 5. First-test execution and scope

Dividend-adjusted prices are accepted for a first backtest *to verify the pipeline
and to study returns*. They are not evidence of executable performance:

- Past prices are lower than what traded, so share counts, notionals and any
  price-level rule are not historical. Percentage moves are approximately
  preserved.
- There is no bid, ask or spread. No commission, spread or slippage is charged.
  Results are optimistic by those costs, and =run.json= says =costs: none=.
- Dividends are already in the prices; the engine credits none.

Such a run is marked, and cannot be started by accident:

- A backtest on trade-only or adjusted data is rejected unless the request
  explicitly accepts a research-only run.
- The engine, from the data metadata and market rules, records
  ="evidence": {"class": "research-only", "reasons": [...]}= in =run.json= from
  run creation, so failed runs keep it, and in the run result returned over
  REST. The reasons for this scope are =adjusted-prices=, =no-quotes= and
  =costs-none=. A caller cannot set or clear it.
- Consumers, Investor included, must not rank or compare a research-only result
  as equivalent to a run on quote data. Trader cannot enforce that downstream,
  so Investor needs its own change before it reads these runs.
- The absence of the field, as on existing FX runs, does not certify that a
  result is executable.

Engine rules for this source, applied only to runs on it:

| Topic       | Rule                                                                 |
|-------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Instrument  | SPY as an ETF settling in USD, multiplier 1                          |
| Basis       | preserve =Trade=; =OHLC()= selects it, never relabel it =Mid=    |
| Tick        | 0.00000001, so adjusted prices are never rounded                     |
| Size        | whole shares, minimum 1                                              |
| Leverage    | margin rate 1 (cash only); risk per trade unchanged (ADR-093)        |
| Short       | an opening short is rejected: no borrow, locate or dividend model    |
| Fills       | existing rules: a decision at the close fills no earlier than the next bar's open; a gap through a stop fills at the open; the intrabar assumption of the broker applies |

Allowed first scope: one instrument, D1, 2005-02-25 to 2026-09-10, one fixed
long-only strategy with market entries and stops set as a percentage distance. No
dollar thresholds, no parameter optimisation, no multi-instrument or FX mix.

* Compatibility and migration

- =canonical-v2= files, =scheduled-close-v1=, the FX calendar, the known FX holes,
  OANDA builder and validator versions and the default source stay as they are. No
  file is migrated or rebuilt.
- New: the =canonical-trade-v1= *storage* codec, the =non-session= reason
  (valid only in it), the equity availability policy and calendar, and =stooq=
  accepted by the build, read and backtest paths. Both storage codecs decode
  into the *same* =marketdata.Bar=. General consumers continue to use
  =OHLC()=; quote-dependent consumers explicitly check =Bid= and =Ask=.
  Do not introduce a provider-specific or consumer-specific bar type.
- The SPY market is added to the catalog only for =stooq= runs, so the settings
  recorded for an FX run do not change.
- The JSON additions are additive; existing fields keep their meaning.
- Each implementation step keeps the existing FX tests and the EMAX scenario
  checksum unchanged, and adds a test that an OANDA file encodes byte for byte as
  before.

* Consequences

- Two on-disk codecs share the file rules; the shared parts are factored, not
  copied. There is still one canonical =marketdata.Bar= and one =OHLC()= contract.
- Keep the existing value fields and their zero/absence semantics for this
  milestone; investigate pointers/getters only with measurements and a separate
  API decision.
- A backtest range on Stooq is all-or-nothing on one snapshot, and old snapshots
  accumulate.
- 8-decimal columns are wider than SPY needs.
- Symbols with fractional volume or out-of-range prices are not fully loadable yet.
- Follow-up issues, in order: schema and calendar; =provider/stooq= and the SPY
  build; engine market rules; the first backtest with golden output.

* Alternatives considered

- *Add trade columns to =canonical-v2=, or one new superset on-disk schema.*
  Deferred rather than ruled out forever: altering the existing strict reader
  under the same version would break FX compatibility; a new unified version
  would need migration/dual readers. A separate trade encoding is a small
  initial compatibility choice, not a different domain bar.
- *Convert marketdata.Bar into consumer-selected bar types.* Rejected:
  unnecessary duplication, implicit conversions, possible loss of available
  quotes, and unchanged requirements for consumers to understand price basis.
  Use =OHLC()= for ordinary price calculations and explicit optional series for
  consumers that need specific data.
- *Replace optional OHLC values with pointers/private fields now.* Deferred:
  memory and runtime effects must be benchmarked against the cost of allocations
  and a public-API migration. Existing =IsZero()= absence semantics suffice.
- *Write trade prices to =Mid=.* Rejected: it mislabels data (#733).
- *Decimals per instrument.* Rejected: another property to maintain and a blocker
  at expansion, for a few bytes saved.
- *Midnight UTC daily bars (ADR-049).* Rejected: =Time= would precede the session
  by hours and say nothing about when the bar may be used.
- *Always make a bar available at 16:00 or later, with no early-close list.* Safe
  and simpler, but #733 asks for early closes to be handled.
- *Computed holiday rules.* Rejected: unscheduled closures need a list anyway.
- *Wait for unadjusted prices with dividend events before any backtest.* Better
  evidence, but no such data is on hand. It stays the requirement for a result
  meant to support trading.

* Owner decisions

Approved 2026-11-26:

1. =canonical-trade-v1= is added as a storage encoding, preserving one
   =marketdata.Bar=, =OHLC()= selection and the existing =canonical-v2= FX files.
2. Prices have 8 decimals; fractional volume is rejected for now.
3. A series is one snapshot, with no override.
4. =Time= is 09:30 and =AvailableAt= the scheduled close, from an embedded date
   table.
5. Strict loads keep failing on SPY 2011-02-17 until it is confirmed; only then
   is a known-hole entry considered.
6. Dividend-adjusted prices are accepted for the first test under the limits
   above, as an explicitly requested, marked research-only run.
7. Opening shorts in equities are rejected.
