#+title: ADR-004 Exact Numeric Representation
#+startup: overview

* Status

Accepted

Parent decision: [[https://github.com/rustyeddy/trader/issues/19][#19 — M1-01 Decide exact numeric representation]]

Accepted after resolution of issues #33 and #35 through #43.  Issue #44 records
the final consolidation and acceptance review.

* Context

Trader must represent authoritative prices, money, quantities, rates,
balances, fees, margin, order values, fill values, and PnL without relying on
binary floating-point approximation.

The same values must behave consistently across:

- backtesting;
- historical replay;
- paper trading;
- live execution;
- broker and provider adapters;
- configuration and command-line input;
- persistence, journals, events, and reports.

Trader is intended to support more than FX.  The representation must cover the
intended requirements of equities, ETFs, futures, crypto assets, fractional
quantities, high-priced instruments, small fees, and financing rates.

The legacy =trader-first-try= repository contains useful fixed-point concepts,
algorithms, tests, and operational lessons.  Under ADR-013 it is a code donor
and behavior reference, not a dependency of the new Trader.

* Decision

** Semantic numeric types

Trader defines distinct foundational semantic types:

- =Price=;
- =Money=;
- =Quantity=;
- =Rate=.

The public API does not expose one generic decimal type in place of these
semantic types.  Distinct types prevent accidental substitution of prices,
quantities, monetary amounts, and dimensionless rates.

=Currency= is a separate domain concept owned directly by =Money=.

** Common authoritative representation

The authoritative numeric amount for =Price=, =Money=, =Quantity=, and =Rate=
is a signed =int64= scaled by =1e8=.

A decimal value =v= is represented as:

#+begin_example
raw = v * 100_000_000
#+end_example

The quantum is:

#+begin_example
0.00000001
#+end_example

The exact representational range is:

#+begin_example
minimum raw:    -9,223,372,036,854,775,808
maximum raw:     9,223,372,036,854,775,807
minimum scaled: -92,233,720,368.54775808
maximum scaled:  92,233,720,368.54775807
#+end_example

The selected scale is an internal representation detail.  It is not an
instrument tick size, display precision, public wire-format scale, or provider
quotation convention.

** Price

=Price= represents an authoritative instrument price.

- Backing amount: signed =int64= at scale =1e8=.
- Normal domain values are non-negative.
- Zero is representable; the surrounding operation determines whether zero is
  valid.
- Negative construction is rejected where the domain requires a market price.
- Tick size, permitted increments, and display precision are instrument rules,
  not properties of the numeric scale.

Price increment validation is exact when price and tick size are represented in
the same scale:

#+begin_src go
priceRaw % tickRaw == 0
#+end_src

Provider-specific quotation conventions such as Treasury 32nds, grain
fractions, grouped thousands, and scientific notation are normalized by the
provider adapter before reaching the exact decimal parser.

** Money and currency ownership

=Money= is an immutable semantic value containing:

- a signed scaled =int64= amount at scale =1e8=; and
- a required =Currency= denomination.

Conceptually:

#+begin_src go
type Money struct {
    amount   int64
    currency Currency
}
#+end_src

Rules:

- Same-currency amounts may be compared, added, and subtracted using checked
  arithmetic.
- Cross-currency arithmetic and comparison are rejected.
- Currency is never silently assumed, dropped, or coerced.
- Currency conversion is an explicit domain operation requiring a target
  currency, conversion rate, and provenance.
- =Money{}= is invalid because it has no currency.
- Valid zero money is constructed explicitly for a denomination.
- Fees, balances, realized PnL, unrealized PnL, and other monetary values remain
  self-describing across domain and external boundaries.
- Homogeneous internal containers may share one currency across raw amounts as
  an optimization, but that does not replace the domain-facing =Money= value.

** Quantity

=Quantity= uses signed =int64= storage at scale =1e8=.

Rules:

- Public quantities are non-negative.
- Direction is represented by =Side=, never by quantity sign.
- Zero is representable and valid for states such as a flat position or fully
  closed lot.
- Order construction normally requires a quantity greater than zero.
- Negative public order quantities are rejected.
- The maximum whole-unit quantity is approximately =92,233,720,368=.
- Quantities above the supported range are rejected, never truncated or
  silently rescaled.

Instrument and broker rules remain separate from representation:

- quantity increment;
- minimum order size;
- maximum order size;
- integral-only requirements.

The initial domain does not support positions above the =1e8=-scaled =int64=
range merely to accommodate extreme high-supply-token pressure cases.  A
future concrete provider requirement may reopen that boundary.

** Rate

=Rate= is the foundational exact dimensionless numeric type.

- Backing amount: signed =int64= at scale =1e8=.
- The representation is signed.
- Zero validity and semantic bounds depend on use.
- Domain APIs may introduce semantic wrappers where direction, bounds, or
  interpretation would otherwise be ambiguous.

Examples include percentages, financing rates, conversion rates, ratios, and
bounded oscillators.

** Numeric type decision matrix

| Type     | Backing | Scale | Domain range and sign policy                            | Zero policy                             | Related metadata                                       |
|----------+---------+-------+---------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| Price    | int64   |   1e8 | Normally non-negative; max 92,233,720,368.54775807      | Representable; context decides validity | Tick size and display precision belong to instrument   |
| Money    | int64   |   1e8 | Signed; full scaled-int64 amount range                  | Valid only with explicit currency       | Currency is owned by Money                             |
| Quantity | int64   |   1e8 | Public values non-negative; max 92,233,720,368.54775807 | Representable; orders normally require >0 | Increment and limits belong to instrument or broker  |
| Rate     | int64   |   1e8 | Signed; semantic bounds depend on use                   | Domain-dependent                        | Semantic constraints belong to wrapper or operation    |

* Arithmetic Policy

** Checked authoritative arithmetic

All arithmetic on authoritative Trader numeric values is checked.

Operations report errors for:

- overflow;
- underflow;
- division by zero;
- results outside the semantic domain;
- results that cannot be represented under the selected rounding policy.

Authoritative arithmetic never silently:

- wraps;
- saturates;
- clamps;
- discards overflow;
- or converts through =float64= to escape fixed-point range constraints.

Normal public operations return errors.  =Must...= helpers may panic only for
programmer-controlled constants, fixtures, tests, and equivalent trusted
inputs.

** Default rounding

The default rounding mode for arithmetic results that cannot be represented
exactly at scale =1e8= is:

#+begin_example
round to nearest, ties to even
#+end_example

Operations requiring another policy must select and document it explicitly.
Supported named policies include:

- round to nearest, ties to even;
- round toward zero;
- round down, toward negative infinity;
- round up, toward positive infinity.

Implicit integer truncation is not an undocumented rounding policy.

** Addition, subtraction, negation, and absolute value

Addition and subtraction use checked =int64= arithmetic.

Negation and absolute value reject =math.MinInt64= because its positive
counterpart is not representable by signed =int64=.

** Scaled multiplication and division

Operations involving two scaled operands use a widened signed intermediate
before restoring the common =1e8= scale.

Examples include:

- =Price * Quantity=;
- =Price * Rate=;
- =Money * Rate=;
- =Quantity * Rate=;
- =Rate * Rate=;
- corresponding scaled division operations.

Conceptually:

#+begin_example
result = round((a * b) / scale)
#+end_example

The intermediate product is wider than =int64=.  Production may use
=math/bits= or an internal signed-128 abstraction.  The widened representation
is not part of the public numeric API.

Arithmetic may be reordered only when the reordered operation is
mathematically equivalent, exact under the documented rounding policy, and
proven not to introduce overflow or precision loss.

** Operation boundaries

Valid foundational operations include:

- same-type comparison;
- checked addition and subtraction where semantic units are compatible;
- =Price * Rate -> Price=;
- =Money * Rate -> Money=;
- =Quantity * Rate -> Quantity=;
- division of compatible like values to produce =Rate=;
- =Price * Quantity= only with the instrument and currency context needed to
  interpret the resulting notional value.

Intentionally absent operations include:

- implicit arithmetic between unlike semantic types;
- addition or comparison of unlike currencies;
- implicit currency conversion;
- negative public order quantities;
- using quantity sign to encode buy or sell;
- implicit tick-size or quantity-increment rounding.

** Accumulation

Finite domain totals use checked addition.

Potentially long-running analytical accumulations use either:

- a widened accumulator; or
- a bounded mathematically equivalent formulation such as a running mean.

Potentially unbounded series do not use unchecked =sum += value= accumulation.
Overflow is detected and reported at the operation where it occurs.

* Parsing and Formatting Policy

** Exact decimal input

Decimal text is the preferred exact external boundary representation whenever
the transport or provider supports it.

Decimal text is parsed directly into the semantic type's scaled =int64=
representation.  Exact parsing never uses =float64= as an intermediate.

Examples include:

- broker APIs that provide decimal strings;
- configuration files;
- command-line input;
- journals;
- CSV fields;
- tests and fixtures;
- Trader-controlled JSON contracts.

Exact parsers reject:

- malformed input;
- exponent notation unless an adapter first expands it;
- grouped or locale-dependent numbers;
- excess precision beyond eight decimal places;
- values outside the supported range;
- values violating semantic-type invariants.

Exact parsing never silently rounds.

Signed exact parsers support the full signed =int64= range, including
=math.MinInt64=.  Implementations must not create a one-value gap by first
constructing an unrepresentable positive magnitude and only then applying a
negative sign.

** Raw scaled construction

Construction from raw scaled =int64= values is reserved for:

- trusted internal code;
- persistence reconstruction;
- narrowly scoped low-level packages;
- tests explicitly exercising representation boundaries.

Such APIs clearly identify the input as already scaled.  Raw scaled
construction is not the ordinary external integration API.

** Floating-point adapter boundaries

Some external providers and libraries require or return binary floating-point
values.

When a =float64= boundary is unavoidable:

- conversion is isolated to the provider or transport adapter;
- conversion uses an explicit rounding policy;
- NaN is rejected;
- positive and negative infinity are rejected;
- out-of-range values are rejected;
- the conversion is visibly named as rounded or lossy;
- the semantic value is validated before entering the domain.

=float64= is never used as an unnecessary intermediate when decimal text is
available.

** Canonical formatting

Canonical formatting emits normalized plain decimal text directly from the
scaled integer representation.

Canonical output:

- does not use scientific notation;
- does not use thousands separators;
- contains no unnecessary trailing zeros;
- does not end with a decimal point;
- normalizes negative zero to =0=;
- does not convert through =float64=;
- round-trips to the identical raw scaled value.

Examples:

#+begin_example
123.45000000 -> 123.45
1.00000000   -> 1
0.00000001   -> 0.00000001
0            -> 0
#+end_example

Fixed-precision, instrument-specific, and user-facing formatting is a separate
presentation operation.  It may apply an explicit rounding policy and does not
change the stored authoritative value.

* Serialization and Persistence Policy

** Trader-controlled JSON and event payloads

Trader-controlled public wire formats serialize =Price=, =Quantity=, and
=Rate= as canonical decimal strings.

Example:

#+begin_src json
{
  "price": "1.08473",
  "quantity": "1000",
  "rate": "0.005"
}
#+end_src

=Money= is serialized as a structured value containing both amount and
currency:

#+begin_src json
{
  "amount": "123.45",
  "currency": "USD"
}
#+end_src

Raw scaled integers are not exposed in ordinary public events or APIs.

** CSV and configuration

CSV and Trader-controlled configuration use canonical decimal text.

- Currency is stored in a separate required field where money is represented.
- Currency symbols are not embedded in numeric fields.
- Thousands separators and scientific notation are not emitted.
- Empty means absent only when the schema declares the field optional.
- Zero is emitted as =0=, not as an empty field.

** Database persistence

Authoritative internal databases store scaled values as signed =BIGINT=.

- The common =1e8= scale is defined by schema and migration version.
- Scale is not redundantly stored on every row.
- =Money= stores its currency separately and mandatorily.
- Database reconstruction uses clearly named raw-scaled constructors.
- Authoritative database columns do not use binary floating-point types.

** Compatibility

Public schemas are versioned.

Writers emit canonical decimal text.  Readers may accept equivalent exact
forms, such as trailing zeros, when doing so does not require rounding.

The following are breaking changes once a format is public:

- changing a decimal-string field into a JSON number;
- dropping currency from =Money=;
- changing field meaning, units, or semantic type;
- changing accepted precision or canonical representation incompatibly;
- exposing raw scaled integers without a separately versioned contract.

Changing the internal scale is not externally breaking only when no public
contract exposes raw scaled values.

* Floating-Point Containment Policy

Authoritative Trader calculations use exact semantic numeric types.

Binary floating-point values are prohibited from authoritative:

- domain packages;
- brokerage and execution packages;
- accounting and ledger packages;
- risk and position-sizing packages;
- order, fill, trade, position, and portfolio models;
- persistence models;
- public API models and authoritative events.

=float64= may be used only inside explicitly approved internal analytical
implementations where fixed-point arithmetic would be impractical or provide
no meaningful decision benefit.

Approved implementations:

- accept semantic Trader inputs at their boundary;
- keep floating-point values private to the implementation;
- return semantic Trader outputs where the result has domain meaning;
- apply checked quantization and explicit rounding before returning such
  outputs;
- never store float values in authoritative domain objects;
- never pass unquantized float values into execution, accounting, risk, or
  persistence paths.

Arithmetic mode is selected per implementation, not per invocation.  A caller
does not toggle fixed-point versus floating-point with a runtime flag on each
calculation.

Alternative fixed-point and floating-point implementations may coexist only
inside approved internal evaluation packages.  The numeric representation is
not exposed as a permanent public package boundary.

Approved float boundaries are enforced by package structure and automated
architecture checks.  Exceptions require an explicit architectural waiver.

* Numeric Determinism Guarantees

** Authoritative exact values

For supported platforms, authoritative fixed-point operations are required to
produce bit-identical raw values when given identical ordered inputs and the
same explicit policy parameters.

This guarantee applies to:

- exact parsing;
- canonical formatting;
- checked addition and subtraction;
- widened multiplication and division;
- explicit rounding;
- quantization;
- serialization and persistence reconstruction.

Backtest, replay, paper, and live execution use the same semantic numeric
operations, rounding modes, threshold comparisons, and quantization behavior.

** Analytical floating-point implementations

Sealed analytical =float64= implementations are not automatically promised to
be bit-identical across every processor architecture, compiler version, or
third-party statistical implementation.

A floating analytical result cannot directly become an authoritative order,
price, stop, target, quantity, money amount, risk value, or account update.

Before entering an authoritative decision path, the result is converted into a
validated semantic Trader value or an explicit discrete decision.  Once
quantized into a semantic type, subsequent comparison and execution behavior is
exact and deterministic.

Where cross-platform float reproducibility is required for a specific
implementation, that guarantee must be demonstrated and documented by that
implementation rather than assumed globally.

** Threshold behavior

Decision thresholds are tested at:

- the representable value immediately below the threshold;
- exactly at the threshold;
- the representable value immediately above the threshold.

Tests verify both numeric output and resulting trading decisions, including
crossover timing, entry and exit selection, stops, targets, risk limits, and
position sizing.

** Test expectations

The implementation includes:

- golden tests for canonical parse, format, arithmetic, and serialization
  vectors;
- property tests for algebraic and range invariants where applicable;
- fuzz tests for parser and formatter round trips;
- repeated-run tests for authoritative calculations;
- replay comparisons across backtest, paper, and live-compatible calculation
  paths;
- cross-platform CI coverage for supported operating systems and architectures;
- architecture tests preventing unauthorized =float32= or =float64= use.

* Legacy Trader Code Disposition

Decision source: [[https://github.com/rustyeddy/trader/issues/43][#43]]

The foundational numeric implementation is rewritten cleanly against this ADR.
Legacy numeric packages are not copied wholesale and are not imported as
dependencies.

| Legacy component                              | Disposition        | Rationale |
|-----------------------------------------------+--------------------+-----------|
| Distinct Price, Money, and Rate concepts      | Retain concept     | Semantic separation remains correct |
| Existing concrete numeric type implementations | Rewrite          | Old backing widths, scales, APIs, and invariants conflict with ADR-004 |
| Legacy Price =int32= and 1e5 scale            | Reject             | Insufficient range and precision for the selected multi-asset domain |
| Legacy Money and Rate 1e6 scale               | Reject             | Conflicts with the common 1e8 decision |
| Money without owned Currency                  | Reject             | Violates the self-describing Money decision |
| Float-first constructors                      | Reference only     | Provider adapters may need explicit conversions, but float is not the primary construction path |
| Float-based decimal formatting                | Reject             | Canonical formatting must operate directly on scaled integers |
| Panic-first external constructors             | Reject             | External and market inputs return explicit errors |
| NaN, infinity, and range validation cases     | Adapt tests        | Useful adapter-boundary scenarios |
| Boundary, parsing, and formatting test cases  | Adapt tests        | Scenarios remain useful; expectations are rewritten for ADR-004 |
| Fixed-point indicator implementations         | Port algorithm only | Review individually against semantic APIs, widened arithmetic, and float containment |
| Integer square-root and logarithm helpers     | Reference or adapt | Reuse only after correctness, range, and complexity review |
| Accumulator implementations                   | Reference or rewrite | Must satisfy widened or bounded accumulation policy |
| Legacy Range abstractions                     | Reject unless a concrete consumer justifies them | Do not preserve abstractions without proven domain need |
| Replay and backtest golden vectors            | Reuse selectively  | Valuable for behavioral comparison and determinism testing |

The legacy repository is therefore used to harvest:

- test scenarios;
- edge cases;
- golden vectors;
- mathematical algorithms;
- realistic calling patterns;
- documented failure modes.

The migration pattern is:

#+begin_example
inspect legacy behavior
    -> define the new semantic contract
    -> implement cleanly
    -> compare against useful historical vectors
#+end_example

It is not:

#+begin_example
copy legacy package
    -> modify until old tests pass
#+end_example

* Evidence

The scale and quantity decisions are supported by executable test-only studies:

- =test/internal/numericstudy=;
- =test/internal/quantitystudy=.

These studies cover representative FX, equity, futures, crypto, rate,
precision, range, and intermediate-overflow cases.  Generated tables in the
working decision history are guarded against drift by their test harnesses.

Key findings include:

- =1e5= and =1e6= cannot represent all intended asset precision;
- =1e8= covers the intended eight-decimal maximum while retaining ten times
  the range of =1e9=;
- double-scaled products require widened intermediates at every usable scale;
- naïve =int64= accumulation can overflow within realistic long-running market
  series;
- instrument tick and quantity increments remain exact when represented in the
  common scale but validated separately from representation.

* Consequences

** Positive consequences

- Authoritative financial values do not depend on binary floating-point
  approximation.
- Public APIs distinguish price, money, quantity, and rate at compile time.
- =Money= remains self-describing and prevents accidental cross-currency
  arithmetic.
- Exact values round-trip through parsing, formatting, wire formats, and
  persistence.
- Backtest, replay, paper, and live calculations share one authoritative
  numeric contract.
- Instrument tick size, quantity increment, and display precision remain
  separate from storage scale.
- Float use is constrained structurally rather than by convention alone.
- The old repository can contribute evidence without imposing obsolete
  architecture on the new system.

** Costs and constraints

- Scaled multiplication and division require widened intermediate arithmetic.
- Checked APIs are more verbose than raw primitive arithmetic.
- =Money= carries currency metadata.
- The common =1e8= quantity scale imposes an initial maximum of approximately
  92.2 billion whole units.
- Exact parsing, canonical formatting, and serialization require custom code.
- Analytical code using transcendental and statistical operations needs an
  explicit sealed float boundary or a more complex fixed-point implementation.
- Architecture checks and golden tests become part of the permanent
  maintenance burden.

* Alternatives Considered

** Raw float64 throughout

Rejected.  Binary floating point cannot provide exact decimal broker,
accounting, persistence, and replay semantics for authoritative values.

** One generic decimal domain type

Rejected as the public domain model.  A generic type permits accidental
interchange of prices, money, quantities, and rates.

** Arbitrary precision or a third-party decimal throughout

Not selected for the foundational representation.  It may reduce some decimal
arithmetic concerns, but would add allocation, performance, dependency, and
public-API risks.  A third-party representation must not become the implicit
public contract without a separate ADR.

** Per-type or per-asset scales

Rejected for the initial foundation.  Variable scales increase arithmetic,
serialization, persistence, comparison, and API complexity.  The common =1e8=
scale covers the intended supported domain.

** Scale 1e9

Rejected.  It provides a ninth decimal place not required by the intended
domain while reducing representable range and intermediate headroom by a
factor of ten compared with =1e8=.

** Unscaled integral Quantity

Rejected because Trader supports fractional equities and crypto quantities.

** Widened Quantity solely for extreme token supply

Rejected without a concrete provider requirement.  The initial supported
quantity domain is bounded explicitly instead.

** Fixed-point arithmetic for every indicator

Rejected as a universal requirement.  Authoritative values and execution paths
remain exact, while explicitly approved sealed analytical implementations may
use =float64= under the containment and determinism policies above.

** Exposing raw scaled integers in public APIs

Rejected as the ordinary contract.  It would couple consumers to Trader's
internal scale and make future representation changes externally breaking.

* Implementation Requirements

Implementation issue [[https://github.com/rustyeddy/trader/issues/22][#22]] may choose
package layout, naming, and internal helper structure without reopening this
ADR, provided the observable behavior satisfies this decision.

At minimum, implementation tests cover:

- full positive and negative scaled boundaries where semantically valid;
- rejection of negative =Price= and =Quantity= where required;
- invalid =Money{}= and explicit same-currency zero money;
- same-currency arithmetic and cross-currency rejection;
- addition and subtraction overflow;
- negation and absolute value of =math.MinInt64=;
- widened realistic =Price * Quantity= and =Price * Rate= products;
- division by zero;
- rounding immediately below, at, and above midpoints, including negatives;
- exact decimal parsing with all eight places;
- rejection of excess precision, malformed input, grouping, and exponent text;
- canonical format-parse round trips;
- full =math.MinInt64= parsing for signed semantic types;
- negative-zero normalization;
- rejection of NaN and infinities at float adapter boundaries;
- JSON, text, CSV, configuration, and database round trips;
- mandatory currency preservation for =Money=;
- architecture enforcement of approved floating-point package boundaries;
- repeated-run and cross-path determinism of authoritative decisions.
