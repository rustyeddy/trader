* Summary of MR-04's Findings

Full detail, per-instrument data, and reproduction steps are in
=docs/research/mr-04-baseline-study-results.org= and
=docs/research/mr-04-per-instrument-results.csv=. Summarized here
against MR-08's own required questions:

** Did normalized price deviations exhibit measurable mean reversion?

Asymmetrically and inconsistently, not the clean symmetric effect
MR-01's hypothesis predicted:

- *Negative-Z side (price below its rolling mean)*: no reliable
  reversion in either period. Monotonicity failed outright — moderate
  deviations frequently showed as large or larger a subsequent
  return as extreme deviations, the opposite of what "stronger
  reversion at greater deviation" predicts.
- *Positive-Z side (price above its rolling mean)*: a real,
  cross-sectionally broad (7-9 of 10 pairs) short-horizon (4h-12h)
  negative-drift effect, monotonic within the development period
  (extreme stronger than moderate).

** Did the effect persist out of sample?

Not reliably. The one effect worth naming (positive-Z, short-horizon)
was present in both the 2015-2025 development period and the
genuinely untouched 2005-2015 holdout period at 4h/12h, but its
magnitude shrank in holdout, and it did not survive to 24h/48h in
holdout — precisely where the development period showed its
*strongest* result. A pattern strongest exactly where it was least
tested and weakest exactly where it was genuinely out-of-sample is the
signature of overfitting to already-explored data, not of a robust,
persistent phenomenon.

** Did a minimal strategy capture the effect without parameter tuning?

Not tested — MR-05 was never built. This question does not apply: per
MR-01's own explicit non-goal ("no trading-strategy implementation")
and MR-04's own recommendation, no strategy was implemented against a
phenomenon whose own baseline study had already returned
Weak/Inconclusive.

** Did realistic costs leave a usable edge?

Not formally measured — MR-06 was never built, and no spread/slippage/
fee model was ever applied to these returns (explicitly out of scope
for MR-01 through MR-04). It is still worth recording the qualitative
scale for context: the pooled mean forward returns MR-04 found ranged
roughly 0.001%-0.024% per horizon. Typical realistic round-trip FX
transaction costs (spread plus any commission) for the more liquid
pairs in this basket are commonly quoted on a similar or larger order
of magnitude for a single trade, before any consideration of slippage.
This is a rough scale comparison, not a costed backtest, but it is
consistent with — not a refutation of — MR-04's own conclusion that
the phenomenon is too weak to build on.

*Statistical edge vs. tradeable edge*, per MR-08's own required
distinction:

- *Statistical edge*: a real, descriptive signal-to-noise ratio was
  observed on the positive-Z side at short horizons (roughly -4 to -9
  on the simple mean/(stddev/sqrt(n)) diagnostic MR-04 used — itself
  explicitly not a valid significance test, since overlapping horizons
  and FX's serial dependence violate that test's independence
  assumption). Even taken at face value, "distinguishable from pure
  noise by a simple diagnostic" is the weakest of the three tiers.
- *Tradeable pre-cost edge*: not established. The effect's own
  magnitude is small enough, relative to plausible transaction costs,
  that a costed backtest was not judged worth building — this is a
  documented reason for not proceeding, not a claim that pre-cost
  profitability was tested and failed.
- *Tradeable post-cost edge*: not established, and not expected to be
  positive given the pre-cost magnitude above; never formally tested.

** Did behavior generalize across pairs and periods?

Partially and unevenly. The positive-Z short-horizon effect was broad
across the basket (7-9 of 10 pairs agreeing in sign), but one pair —
USD/JPY — consistently ran in the *opposite* direction in every one of
the eight extreme-positive (period x horizon) cells, plausibly related
to JPY's own well-documented carry-trade/trend character. A basket-wide
rule would need to either accept that one pair actively works against
it or exclude it outright — itself evidence the phenomenon is not
uniform across instruments.

** Major failure modes observed

- No reversion signal at all on the negative-Z side, in either period.
- Monotonicity (stronger deviation -> stronger reversion) held on the
  positive side in development but failed entirely on the negative
  side in both periods.
- The positive-side effect's strength was inversely related to how
  genuinely out-of-sample the data was — strongest in the
  already-explored period, weakest/reversed in the untouched holdout
  at longer horizons. This is the clearest single failure mode: it is
  the textbook shape of a result that reflects fitting to
  already-examined data rather than a real, stable market phenomenon.
- Persistent single-instrument asymmetry (USD/JPY) that a
  basket-uniform rule cannot accommodate without an explicit exclusion.

* Decision

Per MR-08's own three-way decision framework:

** Reject / move on.

The baseline phenomenon is too weak, inconsistent, and
out-of-sample-fragile to justify a second mean-reversion research
phase on the current hypothesis. MR-05 (minimal strategy), MR-06
(transaction-cost sensitivity), and MR-07 (cross-pair/period
robustness) are closed without implementation as a direct consequence
of this decision — closing them is not scope-cutting or abandonment,
it is the correct response to MR-04's own evidence, exactly as MR-01's
own "Not Supported/Weak is a successful research result; it does not
trigger parameter optimization" principle requires.

This is a *reject the current hypothesis* decision, not a *refine with
exactly one new hypothesis* decision. MR-04 already named the one
narrow candidate this study's own data suggests — a short-horizon
(4h-12h), upside-only (positive-Z), ex-JPY variant — and deliberately
did not schedule it as follow-on work, per MR-01/MR-04's shared
discipline against tuning the scope after seeing results. This
document does not change that: the candidate remains documented, not
scheduled. If it is pursued later, it must begin as its own new,
separately specified hypothesis and experiment definition, following
MR-01's own template (frozen parameters, predeclared buckets/horizons,
an honest development/holdout split, explicit non-goals) — not as a
continuation or re-opening of MR-01 through MR-04's already-closed
scope.
