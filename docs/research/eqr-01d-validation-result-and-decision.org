#+TITLE: EQR-01D: SPY RSI(2)/Regime Validation Result and Continue/Reject Decision
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Purpose

This document records the second real run of the frozen EQR-01
phenomenon experiment (issue #320, EQR-01D) — the identical,
unmodified protocol EQR-01C (#319) already ran against development
data, now run against the SPY D1 validation partition — and then
evaluates the frozen Continue/Reject gate from
=docs/research/eqr-01-research-protocol.org= using both the
development and validation results together, exactly as the protocol
requires.

Referenced documents:

- =docs/research/eqr-01-research-protocol.org= — the frozen EQR-01A
  protocol and Continue/Reject gate.
- =docs/research/eqr-01c-development-result.org= — the EQR-01C
  development-partition result this document reuses without
  re-deriving.

No frozen parameter (RSI period, EMA period, bucket boundaries,
horizons, minimum observation count, bootstrap method) was changed
between EQR-01C and this run.

* Reproduction

** Runner

=marketdata/eqr01d_validationrun_test.go=, a =//go:build fullarchive=-
gated test in the =marketdata_test= external test package, mirroring
=eqr01c_devrun_test.go='s own structure exactly (same reasons for that
package placement apply identically here — see that file's doc
comment). It:

1. Filters the source Stooq CSV to rows strictly before 2023-01-01
   (the frozen final-holdout start), stopping the scan entirely at the
   first out-of-range row — the same structural stop-scan mechanism
   PR #321's re-review required for EQR-01C's development boundary, so
   no OHLCV value from any final-holdout row is ever parsed here
   either.
2. Imports that filtered copy (development + validation rows, 2005-
   02-25 through 2022-12-30) via =stooq.Import=.
3. Registers SPY and builds a =*marketdata.Manager= with
   =USEquityCalendar= + =StandardUSEquityHolidays(2005..2026)= — the
   same full frozen protocol span EQR-01C uses.
4. Queries exactly the validation partition (=[2019-01-01,
   2023-01-01)=, half-open) — final holdout is neither ingested nor
   queried anywhere in this run.
5. Runs =analysis.RunRSIRegimeEventStudy= (the exact, unmodified
   EQR-01C capability) over the resulting bars with the frozen EQR-01
   parameters, using a bootstrap seed distinct from EQR-01C's own
   (=Seed1=320, Seed2=2000=, following the same "issue number,
   resample count" mnemonic convention) so the two partitions'
   resampling draws are independent.

Invocation used to produce the numbers below (after temporarily
setting =fullArchiveEQR01DSPYCSVPath= to the real local archive path,
then reverting that edit before committing):

#+BEGIN_SRC sh
go test -tags fullarchive ./marketdata/... -run TestEQR01D_ValidationPartition -v
#+END_SRC

Re-running the identical command a second time reproduced every
reported statistic, including every bootstrap confidence interval,
bit-for-bit.

* Adversarial Review Against the Frozen Continue/Reject Criteria

This section evaluates =docs/research/eqr-01-research-protocol.org='s
own frozen gate, criterion by criterion, using development and
validation together. No criterion or evidence bar is invented or
adjusted after seeing these results.

** Ordered tendency toward higher forward returns as RSI(2) decreases (both partitions independently)

*Development*: B1 and B2 mean returns exceed B5 and B6 at every one of
the five horizons (=docs/research/eqr-01c-development-result.org='s
own "Observed Pattern" section) — a genuine ordered tendency in the
predicted direction.

*Validation*: this does *not* hold. At every horizon, B1's mean return
is negative (1d, 2d, 3d, 10d) or approximately zero (5d), and is
*lower* than B5's and B6's mean return at every single horizon (for
example 1d: B1 -0.000865 vs. B5 0.000954 vs. B6 0.000149; 10d: B1
-0.011519 vs. B5 0.002988 vs. B6 0.003197). B2 is mixed — positive at
four of five horizons but with a tiny sample (n=15) and no horizon
where its confidence interval excludes zero.

*Finding*: the required ordered tendency is present in development but
*reversed* at the single most extreme bucket (B1) in validation. This
is exactly the Reject condition the protocol names explicitly: "a
pattern present in one partition but absent or reversed in the other."

** Extreme buckets (B1/B2) meaningfully stronger than non-extreme (B5/B6), both partitions

Fails in validation for B1 specifically (see above: B1 underperforms
B5 and B6 at every horizon in validation, the opposite of "meaningfully
stronger"). B2 does not clearly outperform B5/B6 in validation either
— its mean exceeds B5's at only two of five horizons (1d, 5d) and its
confidence intervals are wide and always include zero.

** Minimum observation-count adequacy (B1/B2 in both partitions)

*Development*: B1 (88) and B2 (91) both comfortably exceed the 30-
observation minimum.

*Validation*: B1 (20) and B2 (15) both fall *below* the 30-observation
minimum and are explicitly marked =Insufficient=true= in the table
above. This is a second, independent Reject condition the protocol
names explicitly: "insufficient observation counts throughout the
relevant buckets in either partition." The validation partition (4
years, ~1,000 sessions, ~809 usable observations after EMA(200)
warmup) simply produced far fewer extreme-oversold (RSI(2)<10) days
than the 14-year development partition did — SPI's 2019-2022 window
was a less volatile, less frequently-extreme-pullback period on a
per-observation basis for this instrument.

** Confidence-interval width and distinguishability from zero

Every B1 confidence interval in validation includes zero — none show a
mean return distinguishable from zero at the 95% level, at any
horizon. This is the opposite of the Continue criterion ("bootstrap CI
excludes zero for at least B1 at the horizon(s) showing the effect, in
both partitions"): development had exactly one horizon (2d) where B1's
CI excluded zero; validation has none.

** Mean vs. median consistency (outlier sensitivity)

In validation, B1's mean and median diverge substantially and even
disagree in sign at several horizons (for example 1d: mean -0.000865,
median +0.003246; 3d: mean -0.003841, median +0.001687). With only 20
observations, this is consistent with a small sample where a few large
negative outliers pull the mean well below the median — the kind of
outlier sensitivity the protocol's own review checklist explicitly
asks to be examined rather than accepted at face value. This makes
B1's negative mean in validation *less*, not more, interpretable as a
reliable reversed effect; it does not, however, rescue it as
supporting evidence either — the median is close to zero and the
sample is too small to draw any conclusion in either direction, which
is itself consistent with the independent "insufficient count" finding
above.

** Positive-return-rate consistency

B1's positive-return percentage in validation ranges 40%-60% across
horizons — not clearly above 50% the way development's B1 was
(56.8%-65.9% across horizons). No consistent signal here either.

** Horizon consistency vs. isolated-horizon effect

Development's own effect was concentrated at short-to-medium horizons
(clearest CI-excludes-zero result at 2d). Validation shows no horizon
at which B1 or B2 clears the bar — this is not a case of "the effect
moved to a different horizon"; it is absent at every horizon tested.

** Does positive-regime conditioning add evidence consistent with the hypothesis?

In validation, the primary (positive-regime) and secondary (all-
regime) B1 numbers are not clearly more supportive of the hypothesis
under regime conditioning — for example validation B1 10d is negative
in both views (primary -0.011519, secondary -0.016296). Regime
conditioning does not rescue the extreme-bucket result in validation,
unlike its modest effect in development.

** Secondary (all-regime) comparison

Reported above and not promoted to confirmatory evidence, per the
protocol's own requirement. Its B2 numbers are more consistently
positive than the primary view's (for example 1d: secondary 0.008576
vs. primary 0.003489), which if anything argues the positive-regime
condition is doing *less* work in validation than the protocol's
Continue criterion would want to see, not more.

final-holdout row, and the validation query itself additionally never
requested anything at or after 2023-01-01. Final holdout was not
accessed in the production of this document.

* Classification and Decision

** Classification: Not Supported

Per the frozen protocol's three-way scheme
(=docs/research/eqr-01-research-protocol.org=, Interpretation classes):
this is not "Weak / Inconclusive" — it is a clean failure of multiple
independent, explicitly-named Reject conditions simultaneously, not a
borderline or inconsistent-but-suggestive result:

- the required ordered tendency is reversed at the most extreme bucket
  (B1) in validation;
- both extreme buckets (B1, B2) fall below the minimum observation
  count in validation;
- no B1/B2 confidence interval excludes zero in validation, at any
  horizon.

Any one of these is independently sufficient to fail the frozen
Continue bar; all three hold at once here.

** Decision: Reject

The frozen EQR-01 hypothesis — "large-cap US equities in a positive
long-term trend exhibit short-term mean reversion after unusually
sharp pullbacks," as operationalized by RSI(2)/EMA(200)/the six frozen
buckets/the five frozen horizons — is *not supported* by the
combination of development and validation evidence, per the protocol's
own predeclared, un-adjusted criteria.

This is recorded here as the computed application of the frozen,
objective Continue/Reject criteria to the measured evidence. Per this
issue's own AI/agent workflow section, Rusty makes/accepts the final
decision; this document's role is to present that application plainly
enough to be accepted, overridden, or further scrutinized without
relying on chat history.

** What Reject means here

Per the frozen protocol and issue #320's own Reject semantics:

- The negative evidence is recorded above in full, including the
  specific numbers and the specific criteria that failed.
- No rescue is made within this issue: RSI period, EMA period, bucket
  boundaries, horizons, and instrument remain exactly as frozen by
  EQR-01A/EQR-01B/EQR-01C.
- Final holdout is not, and will not be, accessed as a consequence of
  this Reject.
- Any future investigation of a related but different hypothesis (for
  example: a longer or shorter RSI period, a different EMA period, a
  narrower or wider bucket scheme, or a different instrument set) must
  be its own new, separately frozen research specification — not a
  silent revision of EQR-01.

** EQR-01 status

EQR-01 stops here. The milestone's outcome is a recorded negative
result: SPY's development-partition evidence for short-term RSI(2)
mean reversion in a positive EMA(200) regime does not replicate on the
validation partition, both in direction (the extreme-bucket effect
reverses) and in evidentiary adequacy (extreme-bucket sample sizes
collapse below the frozen minimum). No further EQR-01 issue should
proceed on this hypothesis's current operationalization.
