#+TITLE: EQS-01: Baseline SMA(200) + 10% Trailing-Stop Equity Trend Strategy
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Purpose

Issue #335 (EQS-01) asks for Trader's first simple, longer-hold equity
trend-following strategy, implemented as a deterministic, backtestable
baseline through Trader's existing =strategy=/=execution=/=risk=/
=backtest= abstractions — not as a claim that this particular
SMA(200)/10% configuration is a good trading strategy. This document
records the implementation (=strategy/smatrend=) and the required
initial backtest against the canonical SPY D1 dataset.

* Strategy Rules (as implemented)

- Compute a simple moving average over =SMAPeriod= daily closes
  (=indicator.SMA=, no signal possible before it is =Ready=).
- Enter long only on a genuine cross-above: the previous completed
  close was at or below its SMA, and the current completed close is
  strictly above it. Continuously remaining above the SMA never
  re-triggers an entry — only the moment of an actual cross does
  (=crossState=, =strategy/smatrend/crossstate.go=).
- Execution happens on the next tradable session (Trader's own
  next-bar-open fill-eligibility rule, issue #214) — never the same
  close that produced the signal.
- Long-only, one position at a time.
- Once long, maintain a high-water mark from each bar's own High and
  a monotonically non-decreasing trailing stop at
  =TrailingStopPercent= below it (10% by reference configuration),
  expressed as an =order.IntentAdjustStop= the normal execution/risk/
  broker pipeline places and ratchets (issues #336/#337, ADR-054).
- The strategy itself never evaluates its own stop trigger: by the
  time =OnBar= observes a bar, =backtest.Scheduler='s own
  =IntrabarAdvancer= wiring (issue #338, ADR-055) has already advanced
  the broker's resting-order machinery (ADR-026) against that exact
  bar, using only the stop level a prior bar established — so a
  survived-the-bar position is observed exactly as a survived position,
  with no lookahead.
- Exit happens only through that trailing stop. A close back below the
  SMA does not exit the position on its own.
- After a stop exit, the strategy requires a fresh cross-above (not
  merely "still above the SMA") before re-entering — a structural
  consequence of feeding the cross-state machine unconditionally every
  ready bar, not a separately coded rule.

See =strategy/smatrend='s own package doc comment and
=strategy.go=/=crossstate.go= for the implementation, and its test
suite (=strategy_test.go=, =regression_test.go=) for direct coverage
of every rule above, including warm-up, no-lookahead, gap-through-stop
behavior, and no-automatic-reentry.

* Architecture Notes

Implementing this strategy against real data surfaced two genuine,
narrowly-scoped gaps in the surrounding execution machinery — neither
specific to =smatrend=, both fixed as their own issues before this
backtest could run to completion:

- *Issue #338* (ADR-055): =backtest.Scheduler= never called the
  simulated broker's own per-bar resting-order-trigger machinery at
  all — a documented, deferred gap. Without it, the trailing stop this
  strategy depends on entirely would place and ratchet correctly and
  then silently never fire.
- *Issue #340* (ADR-056): a strategy-computed stop price (90% of an
  arbitrary high-water mark) routinely lands on a sub-cent value that
  =execution= correctly rejected as not tick-aligned. Fixed by adding
  =num.Price.RoundDown=/=RoundUp= and rounding a computed
  =IntentAdjustStop= price to the listing's own tick size in
  =execution=, in the direction that never makes the stop more
  aggressive than what the strategy computed.

Both are recorded as their own ADRs and were merged ahead of this
backtest.

* Initial Backtest

** Dataset and Provenance

- Instrument: SPY (=etf:ARCA:SPY=)
- Interval: D1
- Provider: Alpaca (issue #297/#323, ADR-050/052), via
  =marketdata.Manager=
- Requested span: 2015-01-01 through 2026-09-01
- *Actual data span*: 2018-11-01 through 2026-08-31 (~7.83 years) —
  Alpaca's own free/IEX-tier historical coverage for SPY does not
  extend back to 2015; the monthly partitions before late 2018
  resolved with zero records. This backtest therefore covers the
  actual available window, not the full requested one.
- Dataset revision hashes for every monthly partition are recorded in
  the full Org report (=report.org=, generated alongside this
  document — see "Reproducing this run" below); each is a genuine,
  content-derived =marketdata.Manifest= revision, not a placeholder.

** Configuration

- =SMAPeriod=: 200
- =TrailingStopPercent=: 0.10 (stop rests at 90% of the high-water
  mark)
- =RiskFraction=: 0.01 (1% of equity risked per trade)
- =AdverseDistance=: $20 (fixed, non-optimized sizing input — EQS-01
  explicitly puts sizing optimization out of scope; this is in the
  right neighborhood for SPY's own price level over the run, not
  tuned to it)
- Starting capital: $100,000
- Fill model: real next-bar-open canonical prices (no slippage/
  commission model configured — "none"/"none")

** Interpretation

Over the actually available ~7.83-year window (which happens to
include the 2022 bear market, the 2023-2024 recovery, and the 2025
volatility), this baseline configuration is dramatically outperformed
by simply buying and holding SPY. Its trailing stop repeatedly exits
during ordinary corrections (three of five trades lose money, average
loss exceeding average win frequency), then requires a fresh cross
above a 200-day average to re-enter — losing much of the subsequent
recovery before re-entering. This is exactly the known weak point of a
long moving-average trend-following system with a comparatively tight
trailing stop in a market that spends much of its time in a strong
secular uptrend: it protects capital during real drawdowns but gives
back a large share of the recovery waiting for a fresh signal.

This is not a claim that trend-following or SMA/trailing-stop systems
are broken — only that *this specific, untuned baseline configuration*
underperforms buy-and-hold over *this specific, data-limited window*.
Per EQS-01's own explicit scope, no parameter optimization (SMA
length, trailing-stop percentage, or sizing) was performed, and none
should be inferred as a next step from this result alone without a
separate, explicitly-scoped research issue.

=~/.config/alpaca/profiles/paper.yaml= (=api_key=/=secret_key= fields
