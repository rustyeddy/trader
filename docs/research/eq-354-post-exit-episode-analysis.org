#+TITLE: EQ-354: Post-Exit/Whipsaw Episode Analysis (SMA Long Hold)
#+STARTUP: overview indent

* Purpose

Issue #354 asks, before changing =strategy/smatrend='s re-entry logic
again, for a diagnostic study of every completed exit/re-entry episode
in the current SMA Long Hold ("probation-trend") playbook run against
real SPY history: what did the market actually do while the strategy
sat flat, and how much of a stop-out's own defensive value was given
back waiting to re-enter. This is diagnostic only — no strategy
parameter or rule changed as part of this issue (per its own explicit
scope boundary).

* Method

One continuous backtest, not a walk-forward sweep: SPY's full Stooq
D1 history (2005-02-25 through 2026-09-04), =strategy/smatrend= at its
current reference "SMA Long Hold" configuration —
=smatrend_probation_walkforward_fullarchive_test.go='s own already-
established baseline:

- ~SMAPeriod~: 200
- ~ExitRuleName~: ~"probation-trend"~
- ~InitialStopBelowSMA~: 0.01
- ~TrailActivationGain~: 0.05
- ~TrailingStopPercent~: 0.10
- ~ReEntryRuleName~: ~"reclaim-exit-price"~

Position sizing is conservative and fixed (~RiskFraction~: 0.01,
~AdverseDistance~: $20, never re-anchored) — this diagnostic reads only
each trade's entry/exit price and timestamp, never its quantity or
PnL, so sizing realism is irrelevant as long as it never distorts a
fill price. A real =journal.Recorder= is wired so each trade's own
entry/exit fill price can be reconstructed (=order.Trade= stores only
=EntryFillIDs=/=ExitFillIDs=, never price directly).

An *episode* is one closed trade immediately followed by another
entry, whichever fate that next entry itself eventually meets. A final exit
with no subsequent re-entry before the dataset ends is not a
"completed" episode and is excluded (issue #354's own acceptance
criterion). Post-exit low is the lowest Low from the exit bar itself
through the bar immediately before re-entry, inclusive of the exit bar
— giving a well-defined reference point even for a same-day
reclaim with zero flat bars between exit and re-entry.

Classification uses one simple, deliberately un-tuned threshold (issue
#354's own instruction): *whipsaw* if the further decline after exit
was 1% or less; *good defensive exit* otherwise.

Candidate re-entry rules (~above-sma~, ~reclaim-exit-price~,
~breakout-N~ for N in {2,3,5,10}, ~rebound-X%~ for X in {2%,3%,5%})
are each evaluated as a bare trigger condition over the bars the
strategy was actually flat, with no strategy code changed and no
hypothetical trade re-simulated — only the date each rule's own
condition first would have fired is recorded, for comparison against
what actually happened. Every candidate omits the real
~Strategy.onFlat~'s own central SMA regime gate (a configured
~ReEntryRule~ is only ever consulted once price is already back above
the SMA); this explains why even ~reclaim-exit-price~ itself — the
rule actually governing this run's real re-entries — sometimes appears
to trigger earlier in this analysis than the real strategy's own
re-entry.

Full reproduction: =marketdata/smatrend_probation_episode_fullarchive_test.go=
(build-tagged ~fullarchive~, run via =go test -tags fullarchive=, with
its own local output-directory/CSV-path constants edited locally —
never committed, matching every other ~fullarchive~ research driver in
this repository).

* Headline Results

37 completed exit/re-entry episodes.

| Metric                                    |    Value |
|--------------------------------------------+----------|
| Good defensive exit                       | 25 (67.6%) |
| Whipsaw                                   | 12 (32.4%) |
| Declined >1% after exit                   | 25 (67.6%) |
| Declined >2% after exit                   | 18 (48.6%) |
| Declined >5% after exit                   |  8 (21.6%) |
| Declined >10% after exit                  |  5 (13.5%) |
| Re-entry above prior exit price           | 35 (94.6%) |
| Rebound given back before re-entry, median |     3.4% |
| Rebound given back before re-entry, mean  |     9.6% |

Two findings jump out immediately:

1. *Most exits are genuinely defensive.* Two-thirds of exits are
   followed by more than 1% of further decline, and over a fifth are
   followed by a real (>5%) further decline. Whipsaws — where price
   barely dipped, if at all, before resuming — are the minority
   (32.4%), not the dominant pattern.
2. *Re-entry is almost never a discount.* 35 of 37 episodes (94.6%)
   re-enter at a price above the level the strategy exited at. This is
   partly structural — ~reclaim-exit-price~'s own trigger condition is
   literally "close back above the exit price" — but it means the
   playbook essentially never buys back in cheaper than it sold, even
   after a real, substantial decline.

** The rebound-given-back distribution is dominated by two episodes

Median rebound given back (3.4%) and mean (9.6%) disagree by nearly 3x
— a strong signal of a right-skewed distribution, confirmed directly:
two episodes account for the overwhelming majority of both "days flat"
and "rebound given back" in the whole 21-year sample.

*** Episode 11: 2007-12-27 exit -> 2012-09-07 re-entry (1716 days flat)

Episode chart (rendered via the new =chart= package, ADR-058):
=/srv/trading/sweeps/smatrend-episode-analysis/highest-rebound-given-back-episode-11.png=
— not committed (private research output, this repository's own
absolute path is arbitrary/environment-specific); regenerate locally
from the CSV/JSON artifacts below.

- Exit $113.81 -> post-exit low $51.77 (2009-03-06) -> re-entry
  $114.53.
- Further decline after exit: *54.5%* — this stop genuinely avoided
  the entire 2008-2009 financial crisis.
- Rebound given back before re-entry: *121.2%* of the post-exit low —
  SPY had to round-trip almost all the way back to the original exit
  level (nearly five years later) before ~reclaim-exit-price~ let the
  strategy back in.
- Classified ~good-defensive-exit~ (correctly — this was an
  excellent exit), but it is simultaneously the single largest source
  of "waited too long to re-enter" in the whole dataset.
- The ~above-sma~ candidate would have triggered 2008-05-19 — *1571
  days earlier* than the actual re-entry. But this is a cautionary
  result, not a promising one: 2008-05-19 sits inside the
  2008 bear-market rally, well before the crash's much larger second
  leg down through March 2009. An ~above-sma~ re-entry here would have
  been a severe whipsaw into the worst of the decline — visible
  directly on the episode chart. This is exactly the kind of result
  issue #354 asks this diagnostic to surface before any rule is
  adopted, not after.

*** Episode 33: 2022-04-11 exit -> 2023-06-13 re-entry (428 days flat)

- Exit $417.91's near-cousin, later in the sample: further decline
  *20.4%*, rebound given back before re-entry *26.1%*.
- Same shape as Episode 11 at smaller scale: a real, substantial
  defensive exit, followed by a long wait for ~reclaim-exit-price~ to
  clear.

Removing just these two episodes from the mean-rebound-given-back
calculation would collapse the mean far closer to the median —
confirming that the rebound this playbook gives back before
re-entering is concentrated in a small number of long flat periods
after major declines, not spread evenly across all 37 episodes (issue
#354's own research question 5). This diagnostic does not compute
per-episode P&L or CAGR (it deliberately reads only entry/exit price
and timestamp, not quantity — see Method), so this finding is
specifically about the rebound-given-back metric, not a direct measure
of foregone CAGR; a real accounting of "lost CAGR" would need its own
controlled backtest with real position sizing (PR #359 review).

** Candidate re-entry signals: earlier, but not necessarily better

| Candidate           | Triggers earlier | Mean days earlier (when earlier) |
|----------------------+-------------------+------------------------------------|
| ~reclaim-exit-price~ (actual rule, pure condition) | 18/37 | 36.7 |
| ~rebound-2%~        |             19/37 |                              132.7 |
| ~breakout-2~        |             19/37 |                              128.4 |
| ~breakout-3~        |             16/37 |                              149.5 |
| ~rebound-3%~        |             15/37 |                              163.1 |
| ~breakout-5~        |             10/37 |                              227.2 |
| ~rebound-5%~        |             10/37 |                              238.2 |
| ~breakout-10~       |              7/37 |                              318.7 |
| ~above-sma~         |              5/37 |                              375.8 |

Two observations:

1. *~reclaim-exit-price~ vs. its own real behavior*: even stripped of
   the SMA regime gate, the bare condition only fires earlier than the
   real strategy in 18/37 episodes, and by a comparatively modest 36.7
   days on average — the gate is doing real, but not dominant, work.
2. *Tighter/faster candidates have the largest average lead time, not
   the smallest* — because they are the ones most likely to fire
   during the two long, deep-drawdown episodes above, where "earlier"
   can mean over a year earlier, deep inside a still-unresolved
   decline. A large mean-days-earlier number is not on its own evidence
   that a candidate is a good re-entry signal; Episode 11's own
   ~above-sma~ trigger is the clearest cautionary example in this
   dataset.

This is exactly the caution issue #354 itself anticipated ("do not
tune classification thresholds against final performance," "compare
conceptually... do not fold them into one optimized strategy in this
issue") — before any of these candidates becomes a real experiment,
each one needs its own controlled backtest with real fills and real
risk, not just an earlier trigger date.

* Answers to Issue #354's Own Research Questions

1. *How often does price continue lower after exit by more than
   1%/2%/5%/10%?* 67.6% / 48.6% / 21.6% / 13.5% of episodes,
   respectively (see Headline Results).
2. *How often is the eventual re-entry above the prior exit price?*
   94.6% (35/37).
3. *How much rebound from the post-exit low is typically surrendered
   before re-entry?* Median 3.4%, but a heavily right-skewed
   distribution (mean 9.6%) dominated by two episodes (see below).
4. *What fraction of exits are obvious whipsaws versus useful
   defensive exits?* 32.4% whipsaw, 67.6% good defensive exit by this
   analysis's own simple, un-tuned threshold.
5. *Is most of the lost CAGR concentrated in a small number of long
   flat periods / sharp rebounds?* This diagnostic cannot directly
   measure lost CAGR (no per-episode P&L or quantity is computed — see
   Method), but its own rebound-given-back proxy says yes: Episodes 11
   (2007-2012, 1716 calendar days flat) and 33 (2022-2023, 428
   calendar days flat) alone dominate both the days-flat and
   rebound-given-back distributions; every other episode is flat for
   83 calendar days or fewer. Confirming the CAGR question itself
   needs a real, separately sized backtest.
6. *Does a simple earlier signal appear promising before another
   parameter sweep?* Not unambiguously. Every candidate rule triggers
   earlier than the real strategy in a meaningful minority of
   episodes, but the candidates with the largest average lead time do
   so specifically by firing during the two long, still-unresolved
   drawdowns above — Episode 11's own ~above-sma~ trigger (1571 days
   "earlier," landing inside the 2008 bear-market rally) is a concrete
   example of exactly the whipsaw risk a faster signal can introduce.
   ~reclaim-exit-price~ itself, and the smaller breakout/rebound
   windows, are the most plausible next controlled experiments — each
   needs its own real backtest, not just this diagnostic's trigger-date
   comparison.

* Next Steps (Not This Issue)

Per issue #354's own explicit scope boundary, none of the candidate
rules above were folded into an optimized strategy here. Candidate
follow-up experiments, each as its own issue:

- A real backtest of a 2- or 3-bar breakout re-entry, since these show
  meaningfully earlier average re-entry without the ~above-sma~
  candidate's demonstrated whipsaw risk. Note this needs new code: the
  existing =strategy/smatrend/reentryrule.go= ~"breakout"~ rule tracks
  the highest High *since the exit* with no configurable N-bar
  lookback window at all — it is not the same rule this diagnostic's
  ~breakout-2~/~breakout-3~ candidates simulate, and the registry has
  no ~"breakout-2"~/~"breakout-3"~ entries to select (PR #359 review
  caught an earlier draft of this section incorrectly claiming
  otherwise). A real experiment needs a new, N-parameterized
  ~ReEntryRule~ implementation first.
- The same for one or two ~rebound-X%~ thresholds, which are likewise
  not yet an implemented ~ReEntryRule~ and would need one.
- Any such experiment should report full P&L, drawdown, and trade
  count against the same SPY history and the existing
  ~probation-trend~ walk-forward baseline
  (=docs/research= via issue #356/PR #357), not just an earlier
  trigger date.
